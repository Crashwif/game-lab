# Yes Men

A dictator's war room, and you are at the table. Crimson walls under a gold cornice, a portrait of the Leader behind his chair that grows as the room agrees, two flags with a star and the word YES, a DAYS WITHOUT ELECTIONS counter on the left wall, a clock that flips to YEAR 1, and an EXIT sign over the only door. Down the middle, a long table with a runner: at the far end, facing us, the Leader in a peaked cap, aviators and a uniform with a sash; along the sides, six ministers in profile with clipboards and name cards (Minister of Agreement, Secretary of Yes, Chief Nodder, Head of Applause, Vibes Czar, Ministry of Truth (acting)); at the near end, with your back to the camera and a YOU tag bobbing over your head, you. In front of the Leader sits a red TOTAL CONTROL button under a glass dome. Along the foot of the picture, STATE TV runs its chyron.

The multiplier is the agenda. Every step up is a worse decree and the room agrees harder: "rename Tuesday. after me." at the start ("inspired, sir. I never liked Tuesday"), the elections moved to never by 1.15×, the press on a permanent vacation by 1.3×, term limits deleted, his face on the money, a window inspection for the opposition leader by 1.78×, the courts turned into his group chat, the word "no" banned by 2.1×, a sung loyalty oath, dream surveillance, "I invented bread", the neighbours' country annexed "for the vibes" by 3.5×, a four-year-old emperor, forever emergency powers, the Smile Camp at 5×, statues in every living room, the sun by decree, the internet reduced to his speeches, Year One at 11×, "I am the state. also the weather", immortality passed unanimously, gravity banned, the moon summoned, his face on every baby, "I'm god now. minor update" at 60×, and the universe served notice at 100×, after which the meeting re-approves itself every six seconds for as long as the round runs. Your own lines are thoughts, in a cloud ("say something. say something.", "i could just leave. the door is right there."), and they stay thoughts. Every captioned decree is stamped on the pile at his right hand, pins a medal to his chest and runs a headline on the chyron; the caption is the last decree said, so it never runs ahead of the dialogue.

The room's zeal is 1 − 1/x (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×) on a slow spring that every decree kicks. The ministers nod faster and deeper, start the seal clap around 1.5×, sweat from about 2×, rise for a standing ovation past 2.2×, weep with joy past 3.3× and go down on their knees past 6×; at 2.62× the Leader asks who stopped clapping first, the Head of Applause goes still with his hand at his collar, two guards come in through the door and march him out of it, and a replacement with a NEW tag walks in and takes the seat. The Leader's cap grows, his chest puffs, his glint brightens, and once eighteen medals have no room left the next ones fall off and bounce on the table. His left hand drifts from the table to the button and trembles over it while the glass dome lifts on its hinge from about 2.3×, with a warning light that blinks faster as it goes. Your shoulders rise and your head sinks between them as it gets worse; on every decree your hand half-rises beside your head to object, then drops. The counter on the wall runs away with the round, the portrait outgrows its frame and the clock flips at Year One. A pulse ticks under the band, quickening with the tension, until you leave or he slams it.

An accepted cash-out is the walk-out. You stand (the chair creaks, the room gasps and every minister turns to look), you say "That's going too far." out loud, and you walk the length of the near side and out of the door, which swings open as you reach it. The Leader says "note the name." and a minister notes it in pen; a loyalist is in your chair three seconds later with a NEW tag, and the chyron says so. The agenda carries on without you while a regret ladder plays against the exit: "his chair is still warm, sir" at 1.5× the exit, "the walker-out is now a former person" at 2×, the door renamed after you at 3× and a statue of the empty chair at 10×; the caption goes from THAT'S GOING TOO FAR to THEY KEPT CLAPPING to YOUR CHAIR WAS FILLED to THEY RENAMED THE DOOR.

The crash is the Leader taking the system. His hand slams the button: a flash, a 0.15 s hit-stop with the camera punched in on him, then in slow motion the decrees and the loose medals fly in a seeded fan, the glass shatters, the lights go red, a banner with his face unrolls down the wall reading ALL SYSTEMS: HIS, the ministers drop to their knees and the multiplier on the HUD is stamped SEIZED while STATE TV becomes HIS TV. At 1.2 s the camera flashes and the picture develops into the historical photo: sepia, a vignette, a white border, a red marker circle that draws itself around your seat with "you" written beside it, and a plaque that reads "The Room, Year One. Everyone agreed. (colourised)". If you were still at the table, YOU'RE IN THE PHOTO and YOU CLAPPED; if you walked out, the circle is around the loyalist in your chair, labelled "not you", and you DODGED THE PHOTO; a spectator WATCHED FROM THE HALLWAY. An instant bust skips the agenda: "skip the agenda." and the slam.

- `scene.ts`: composition in depth order (room, throne, the Leader, the table, the ministers and whoever is walking sorted by nearness, whoever is in your chair, the chair, bubbles, what is in the air, the red light), the tension mapping, the round-phase logic, the walk-out and the regret ladder, the crash choreography (flash, hit-stop, camera punch, slow motion, the photo), the fade back to a fresh table, the audio cues and the HUD.
- `court.ts`: the walls, the portrait, the flags, the counter, the clock, the door, the banner, the table with the name cards, the decree pile and the button under its dome, the agenda and its bubbles, the chyron, the seeded papers and medals, the photo overlay.
- `cast.ts`: the Leader's rig (the uniform, the medals, the stamp hand and the button hand, the face shared with the portrait and the banner), the ministers' profile rigs (nod, seal clap, ovation, kneel, sweat, tears, the escort and the replacement), the guards, and the player from behind and in profile on the walk, with the loyalist on the same rig.
- `ink.ts`: the outline, the meme lettering, text wrapping and the room's geometry. `motion.ts`: springs, easing, deterministic noise and a seeded generator. `portrait.ts`: the narrow-screen composition.
- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here). A brass band that marches harder with the multiplier; the crash is a klaxon. Cues: a pop for every bubble, a thud for the stamp, a coin for every medal, a notification for every headline, a creak as the glass lifts, a clang at Year One, the applause coming in and the ovation's cheer and stomp, a whoosh and a yelp for the escort, the door, a tick on the clap that quickens with the room, the player's heartbeat until the walk-out or the slam, a gasp and the register for the walk-out, the klaxon, the thud and the crowd for the slam, and the camera.
- `main.ts`: the shared page shell. `replay.json`: a verified 9.07× round for the gallery, copied from the Hello World template with this game's ID.

Zeal, the agenda, the captions, the medals, the counter, the dome, the chyron and the HUD all follow the displayed multiplier; the regret ladder follows the multiplier against the accepted exit; nothing drawn here reads or changes the committed outcome. The papers and medals are seeded from the crash point and fly on an exact gravity step, so a replay throws them the same way at any frame rate. A scene that opens mid-round or on a crashed round settles into place: the decrees stamped and the medals pinned, the headlines that ran, the glass as far up as the round has it, the Head of Applause already replaced past 2.62×, the loyalist in the chair if you walked out, or the room red with the photo on the wall. The cut from a crashed round to the next betting window goes through a short fade. The published game uses full animation regardless of browser motion preferences. Sound is opt-in through the Sound button.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents a close-up of the table, a small overview that preserves the room and the door, and the current line from the actual dialogue. The canonical shell still owns controls, round state and accepted cash-outs.

Run `npm run preview -- yes-men` from the repository root and open the printed URL. Join with 50 local credits, then cash out during a running round to walk out. `npm run build` produces the three publishable files in `dist/yes-men/`.

All visuals are original Canvas 2D shapes and text; sound uses the repository's shared procedural helper (`clips.json` is `{}`). The Leader, the country and the ministers are fictional. Credits have no monetary value. No third-party assets are included. The game is an open remix origin (`gallery.json`).
