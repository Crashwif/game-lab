# Tower Tension

A crane stacks prefab floors onto Ponzi Towers, a condo tower that sways more the higher it gets: the developer's billboard on the site fence reads `PONZI TOWERS` / `LUXURY CONDOS · PAY IN $FLOOR`, the crane's counterweight is painted `100X`, and the floor count is the floor price. Residents move into the windows a moment after each floor lands (more of them the higher the stack) and wave harder the worse the sway gets; under the billboard a sales office runs the presale, its banner's APY climbing with the multiplier and a queue of buyers growing with it, one going in for every floor the crane sets. The worker on top is the player: an accepted cashout sells the penthouse to the next guy, calls the hoist and rides him down to safety (the office stamps `PENTHOUSE SOLD` and fires confetti), and the crash releases every joint so the stack tips and breaks apart with him still up there if he waited too long, throwing the residents clear, scattering the queue and flipping the office to `SOLD OUT · NOT OUR PROBLEM`.

- `motion.ts`: the same toolkit as Balloon Pump: exact damped springs, easing, deterministic noise, a seeded generator.
- `audio.ts`: the page's sound (the shell's copy, the same in every game). The scene asks for the `trap` style with a `boom` crash: a sub-heavy beat that tightens with the multiplier, a thud and a `kaching` for every floor that lands and sells, a ratchet on each pickup, a creak as the stack reverses at the end of each sway (louder the further it went), a rising ding and airhorn at the caption's milestones, the register and fanfare of the cash-out (a ding when the cage arrives, the money when the ride ends), and at the crash a creak, the boom, screams as the residents go and thuds as the floors hit.
- `people.ts`: tiny Wojaks: the residents leaning out of the windows, the same people falling with the floors, and the presale queue.
- `tower.ts`: the stack as a shear building: each floor is joined to the one below by a lateral spring with inter-storey damping, integrated with fixed substeps. Landing floors arrive with the hook's swing, wind pushes harder with height, and the bending modes (slower and wider as the tower grows) come out of those springs. Residents arrive in each floor's windows on a spring shortly after it lands, wave faster and wider with the fear, and scream with both hands up near the top, while cracks grow across the two base floors. The collapse tips the attached part about the base like a rigid rod and sheds floors from the top down into a seeded debris fall with ground bounces, dust and rubble; each releasing floor throws its residents clear (capped at 26 in the air), who spin, bounce and end face down in the site dust.
- `office.ts`: the sales office under the billboard: the `PRE-SALE · 420% APY` banner (the number is 420% of the multiplier squared, popping each time it grows a digit), the `OPEN` sign that swings on a spring with every sale, the salesman in the window, the queue (one idler between rounds, up to seven at the top, walking in from the left and shuffling forward as the front buyer goes in on each landed floor), `SOLD` stamps, the `PENTHOUSE SOLD` confetti (40 pieces) of an accepted exit, and the scatter at the collapse.
- `crane.ts`: the tower crane (its counterweight marked `100X`) and its cycle (hook to the pile, lift, trolley out, lower, set, release). The block hangs as a real pendulum under the trolley, so starts and stops leave it swinging and it lands slightly off centre. The counterweight hangs on chains as a second, barely damped pendulum: the wind leans it, the trolley and every landed floor jolt it, and the tension scales all of it, so it is throwing itself about by the top. The crane climbs with the stack.
- `worker.ts`: the worker. He climbs onto each block as it comes down, leans against the sway and flails when it gets bad, calls the hoist on an accepted exit, rides it down and stands clear, or falls with the floors.
- `scene.ts`: the camera (pans and zooms to keep the top in view, follows the hoist down, pulls back for the collapse), the altitude sky (hazy horizon to stars and the moon), parallax skyline and clouds, the site and its billboard, the HUD (captions, multiplier, the floor price, which drops to 0 once the tower is down, the secured badge), the sound cues and the round-phase logic. The crash is choreographed: the base gives with a lurch and a creak, the joints let go a few frames later (the fuse), the picture holds for a 70 ms hit-stop, the first of the fall runs at a third speed, and the camera punches in on the tipping stack and eases back out. A collapse met late (a page opened after it, or a crash missed while the tab was hidden) settles straight into the rubble, residents face down beside it, the queue gone and the office closed, with no effects.
- `main.ts`: SDK connection, round lifecycle, the player's bet and cashout, input controls and the animation loop.
- `replay.json`: a verifiable settled example round (7.27×, seventeen floors) used by the gallery.

One floor lands per sixth of a doubling of the displayed multiplier, so the crane keeps a steady rhythm while the tower's height, sway period, wind, the residents' waving, the counterweight's swing, the queue and the worker's nerves all follow the multiplier. Nothing in the presentation changes the committed outcome. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off the screen shake, the hit-stop, the slow motion, the camera punch-in and the twinkle. Sound is opt-in through the Sound button and stays off until it is pressed.

Run `npm run dev` from the repository root and open the Tower Tension URL it prints. Join with 50 local credits, then cash out during a running round to send the worker down. `npm run build` produces the three publishable files in `dist/tower-tension/`.
