# Tower Tension

A crane stacks prefab floors onto Ponzi Towers, a condo tower that sways more the higher it gets. Every floor arrives from off screen on the flatbed of a semi under an `OVERLEVERAGED LOAD` banner, and the crane lifts it off the bed while the next truck rolls in. The developer's billboard on the site fence reads `PONZI TOWERS` / `LUXURY CONDOS · PAY IN $FLOOR` under an `AUDITED BY MY COUSIN` sticker, the crane's counterweight is painted `100X`, and the floor count is the floor price. Residents move into the windows a moment after each floor lands (more of them the higher the stack), the first one in says so (`GM`, then `WAGMI`, `WEN EXIT` and `NGMI` as it gets worse), and they wave harder the worse the sway gets; under the billboard a sales office runs the presale, its banner's APY climbing with the multiplier and a queue of buyers growing with it, one going in for every floor the crane sets. The worker on top is the player, tagged `YOU` (`ANON` to a spectator): an accepted cashout sells the penthouse to the next guy, calls the `EXIT LIQUIDITY` hoist and rides him down to safety (the office stamps `PENTHOUSE SOLD` and fires confetti), green tape marks `YOU SOLD HERE` on the storey he left while the HUD counts the floors stacked since he jeeted, and the crash releases every joint so the stack tips and breaks apart with him still up there if he waited too long, throwing the residents clear, scattering the queue and flipping the office to `SOLD OUT · NOT OUR PROBLEM`. An exit taken in time reads `DODGED!` with the tape hanging where `FLOOR YOU DODGED` was.

- `motion.ts`: the same toolkit as Balloon Pump: exact damped springs, easing, deterministic noise, a seeded generator.
- `audio.ts`: the page's sound (the shell's copy, the same in every game). The scene asks for the `trap` style with a `boom` crash: a sub-heavy beat that tightens with the multiplier, a thud and a `kaching` for every floor that lands and sells, a ratchet on each pickup, an air-brake hiss as each truck pulls up and its engine as it pulls away, a creak as the stack turns at the end of each sway (louder the further it went, more often with the tension, softer once your exit is in), a creak and a gasp at each fake-out lurch, a rising ding and airhorn at the caption's milestones, the register and fanfare of the cash-out (a ding when the cage arrives, the money when the ride ends), and at the crash a creak, the boom, screams as the residents go and thuds as the floors hit.
- `people.ts`: tiny Wojaks: the residents leaning out of the windows, the same people falling with the floors, and the presale queue.
- `tower.ts`: the stack as a shear building: each floor is joined to the one below by a lateral spring with damping, integrated with fixed 1/240 s substeps. Each storey is stiffer for every floor it carries and the frame stiffens as it grows, so the base stays planted, the top does the swaying, and a tall tower sways slower (about a second a sway on the first floors, several seconds fifty floors up). The wind leans the stack and a sway drive, its phase integrated at a rate that follows the stack's first mode, rocks it from the top; both are sized from the stack's own compliance, so a lone block barely slides on the slab, two floors rock about ±6 px at 1.7×, three about ±10 px and the four floors of 3× about ±20 px, wider through a long round but never past about 120 px (the old springs let a long round drift like a noodle). Landing floors arrive where the crane set them, with the hook's swing. Residents arrive in each floor's windows on a spring shortly after it lands, wave faster and wider with the fear (each on its own integrated phase), and scream with both hands up near the top, while cracks grow across the two base floors from the first floors. A fake-out lurch kicks the stack and the slab coughs dust. The collapse tips the attached part about the base like a rigid rod and sheds floors from the top down into a seeded debris fall with ground bounces, dust and rubble; blocks topple onto their broad face and scrape to a stop rather than snapping flat. Each releasing floor throws its residents clear (capped at 26 in the air), who spin, bounce and topple face down in the site dust. When the next round opens, the rubble fades under its dust.
- `office.ts`: the sales office under the billboard: the `PRE-SALE · 420% APY` banner (the number is 420% of the multiplier squared, popping each time it grows a digit), the `OPEN` sign that swings on a spring with every sale, the salesman in the window, the queue (one idler between rounds, up to seven at the top, walking in from the left and easing forward as the front buyer goes in on each landed floor, their steps following the ground they cover, bouncing on their phones faster with the tension), `SOLD` stamps, the `PENTHOUSE SOLD` confetti (40 pieces) of an accepted exit, and the scatter at the collapse.
- `truck.ts`: the deliveries. Each floor comes in from off screen on the flatbed of a semi: a white cab with a red stripe and the usual sad face at the wheel, the floor strapped down on the bed under an `OVERLEVERAGED LOAD` banner. The truck brakes to a stop under the jib (its nose dips, then rocks back), the bed springs up as the crane takes the weight, and the empty truck pulls away to the left behind the sales office while the next one passes behind the tower from the right. The trucks run on the crane's cycle, so at any pace each one is parked before the hook comes down, and a page that joins mid-round finds the convoy where the cycle puts it. Before a round the first floor's truck drives in on its own clock. At the crash the truck under the crane bolts with whatever is still on its bed, and the ones on their way brake hard, wait and back out.
- `crane.ts`: the tower crane (its counterweight marked `100X`) and its cycle (hook down to the truck, a beat while the slings go on, lift, trolley out, lower, set, release). Empty, the trolley and hook go exactly where the cycle sends them (and hold there through a frame the multiplier stands still), so the hook is over the floor when it takes it; from there the block hangs as a real pendulum under the trolley, the cable and hook drawn along the swing, so starts and stops leave it swinging. The trolley follows the stack's lean and the tag lines steer the block onto the top as it sways, so it lands where the top is, a few px off centre by the floor, and never jumps sideways onto the stack. The counterweight hangs on chains as a second, barely damped pendulum: the wind leans it, the trolley and every landed floor jolt it, and the tension scales all of it, so it is throwing itself about by the top. The crane climbs with the stack, and a new site's cycle starts from wherever the last round left the trolley, hook and counterweight.
- `worker.ts`: the worker. He hops onto each block as it comes within reach, also while he waits for the hoist, leans against the sway and flails when it gets bad, calls the hoist on an accepted exit, walks into it, rides it down and walks clear (the `EXIT LIQUIDITY` cage stays parked at the foot of the rail), or falls with the floors and topples flat where he lands. His legs are two 11 px bones that bend at the knee, and his hips drop when he leans hard or strides wide so they never stretch; his steps follow the distance he covers and ease in and out, so his feet stay planted. A new round finds him picking himself up (once he has landed, after a tall fall) and walking back to the slab, while the parked cage fades out.
- `scene.ts`: the camera (pans and zooms to keep the top in view, follows the hoist down, eases over to frame the collapse and follows the debris), the altitude sky (hazy horizon to stars and the moon), parallax skyline and clouds, the site and its billboard, the `YOU`/`ANON` tag and the worker's speech bubble, the exit tape, the HUD (captions, multiplier, the floor price, which drops to 0 once the tower is down, the secured badge and the floors-since-you-jeeted counter), the sound cues and the round-phase logic. Fake-outs at 1.8×, 2.6×, 4.2×, 7.5× and on up the ladder lurch the stack while the worker calls it `PROBABLY NOTHING` (`GLAD I SOLD` once he is clear); they are keyed to the multiplier only. After an exit the caption runs a regret ladder on how far it ran since (`SOLD TO THE NEXT GUY`, `JEETED WITH PROFIT`, `THE NEXT GUY IS UP BIG`, `PROFIT IS PROFIT`). The crash is choreographed: the base gives with a lurch and a creak, the joints let go a few frames later (the fuse), the picture holds for a 150 ms hit-stop while the camera punches in 10% and holds for 0.3 s, the first of the fall runs at a third speed, and the camera eases back out. Every collapse splits the slab, and a crash before the first floor is up reads `RUGGED AT FOUNDATION`. When the next round opens the secured badge shrinks away and the exit tape fades with the rubble. A collapse met late (a page opened after it, or a crash missed while the tab was hidden) settles straight into the rubble, residents face down beside it, the queue gone and the office closed, with no effects.
- `main.ts`: SDK connection, round lifecycle, the player's bet and cashout, input controls and the animation loop.
- `replay.json`: a verifiable settled example round (7.27×, eight floors) used by the gallery.

One floor lands per third of a doubling of the displayed multiplier (one every three seconds at the platform's pace), so the crane and the trucks keep a steady rhythm. The tension is `1 - 1/x` (0.33 at 1.5×, 0.5 at 2×, 0.67 at 3×, where nearly every round is played): it drives the sway, the cracks, the residents' and the worker's nerves (worried from about 1.7×, screaming by 3×), the counterweight's swing, the queue and the music, while the floor count keeps a long round changing in height, sway period and size, queue and APY. A beat lands every couple of seconds from the start: the pickup, the landing, the caption, the first resident's line, the fake-outs. Nothing in the presentation changes the committed outcome. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off the screen shake, the hit-stop, the slow motion, the camera punch-in and the twinkle. Sound is opt-in through the Sound button and stays off until it is pressed.

Run `npm run dev` from the repository root and open the Tower Tension URL it prints. Join with 50 local credits, then cash out during a running round to send the worker down. `npm run build` produces the three publishable files in `dist/tower-tension/`.
