# Gender Reveal

A backyard at two in the afternoon, the good kind of hot. Paper lanterns on a string from the oak to the garage, a folding-chair semicircle of both families seen from behind, a dessert table with a two-tier cake dyed questionably neutral. A banner over the fence: **BOY OR GIRL — EITHER WAY, WAGMI**. At center, a couple: **Tanner** (jorts, sunglasses on a lanyard) and **Destiny** (sash: MAMA TO BE), either side of the reveal box, plus Destiny's mother **Sheila** running the event like a general, with a clipboard, a walkie-talkie on her belt and a megaphone. A question-mark piñata hangs from the oak. The slideshow projector someone brought sits on the dessert table, pointed at the garage door. The slideshow is the chart.

The twist, held back for one line of the programme: this is a token launch disguised as a reveal party. The "baby" is a token, and the reveal is never a baby. The couple's midwife — or "dev" — is **Kyle**, a guy in scrubs checking a laptop under the dessert table, its glow leaking from under the cloth and its cable running up to the projector. At the center of the party, a white balloon box with a latch. The key is Kyle's: its keychain says **DEV**, and it hangs from Sheila's clipboard until the programme hands it over.

The multiplier is the crowd's certainty. Every beat is keyed to the displayed multiplier, never to the crash point, and the caption is the last captioned line said, so it never runs ahead of the dialogue.

## Direction

The comedy is the collision of a genre (the gender reveal: its over-production, its violence, its certainty) with the only community that out-produces and out-uncertains it. The reveal box never opens until the crash, and the piñata was never a piñata. Every guest is drawn as a generic cartoon; no real person's likeness, no insignia, no fire or gore — the smoke is theatrical fog, comedy only.

- Sheila speaks from the clipboard, megaphone up (it feeds back), everything a procedure.
- Tanner and Destiny are a negotiation. Their lines are couple-speak; their eyes are the market: as the tension rises their pupils keep drifting to the chart on the garage.
- Kyle is the dev. He is calm because he is working.
- The piñata ticks. Nobody claims it.

## Dialogue ladder

Keyed to the displayed multiplier. Each line is a speech bubble with the speaker's name under it and a cue, queued so a bubble stays up at least 2.6 seconds before the same speaker's next line replaces it. Chat members speak in chat-coloured bubbles over the garage door, and their lines are posted to the projected group chat.

- **Betting:** Sheila, megaphone: "Phones away. Phones AWAY. If it leaks before the reveal, I will end this family." (Leak means pre-launch announcement.)
- **1×:** Sheila: "Programme item one: welcome, guests. GM. Phones stay AWAY."
- **1.2×:** Tanner takes the mic: "Is it on? Okay. We are so blessed. We are SO blessed." Destiny's smile is a negotiation (thinking: "smile. he has the mic. smile."), and every few seconds it twitches at the corner.
- **1.5×:** Sheila: "Grandpa Hal will hold the box key. For the honor." The key leaves her clipboard for Grandpa Hal's fist, held up in a vice grip with the DEV tag swinging under it; his cap says VETERAN, of a war he may have fought in the parking lot. Hal: "I'm keeping it. I've seen combat. Parking-lot combat." First omen.
- **1.8×:** The cake's frosting starts sweating; the neutral dye breaks down into pink on one side and blue on the other. Destiny: "Mom. The cake is… separating." Sheila: "The frosting is NEUTRAL. I signed off on neutral." The cake is front-running the reveal.
- **2.2×:** Kyle pokes his head out from under the tablecloth: "Traction's looking great, babes." Destiny catches him: "Did you invite the scrubs guy?" Tanner: "We don't know him, he came with the bounce house." On the garage door, the family group chat scrolls faster: AUNT CAROL: ??? COUSIN BRAD (from the crypto side of the family): loaded.
- **2.7×:** The piñata starts ticking. Nobody hung a ticking piñata. Sheila: "Whose piñata is that? WHOSE PIÑATA IS THAT." The folding chairs: "…not ours." At 3×, AUNT CAROL: "why is there a chart on the garage".
- **3.3×:** Grandma Pauline (Destiny's side) and Grandma Bev (Tanner's side) walk in from opposite ends of the dessert table and take hold of the plate with the last piece of cake. They lock eyes. "That is my piece." "That is MY piece." Stalemate.
- **4×:** The projector auto-focuses: the chart grows past the garage door and is now bigger than the banner. The banner's middle pins pop and it sags. Sheila: "Note: the chart is now bigger than the banner. EITHER WAY is unpinnable." At 4.5×, Destiny: "Babe. Your eyes are on the garage."
- **5×:** Tanner's phone rings — his boss — and he declines it without looking, eyes still on the chart: "My boss. Declined. Today we are diamond hands, babe." That is when Sheila knows: "…The latch is warm. Why is the latch warm." From here the latch glows and heat shimmers off the box. At 6.4×, COUSIN BRAD: "aped the reveal. no take-backs".
- **8×:** Kyle has stopped pretending. He is on a stool behind the dessert table, headset on, laptop open: "We're live in five. Keep the confetti warm." Worker 2, from behind the bounce house: "Copy that. Confetti warm." At 10×, Tanner: "Babe, why is the scrubs guy on a headset?"
- **13×:** Uncle Dale (a thanksgiving-uncle cameo: the camo cap, a palm-print shirt this time) rises over the fence line: "IT'S A DOG. IT'S ALWAYS A DOG." He is right. He is always right. Grandpa Hal has fallen asleep holding the key ("zzz… key… mine… zzz"), and the piñata now ticks in morse. At 17×, Sheila: "The piñata is ticking in morse. Who taught the piñata morse."
- **25×:** Destiny's water — a bottle of sparkling water — goes over on the dessert table: "My water!" The folding chairs jolt: "*GASP*". The crowd gasps at the wrong thing. At 35×, Kyle: "Worker 2, positions. Fog on my mark."
- **60×:** The balloon box starts levitating slightly, wobbling over its shrinking shadow. Sheila: "The box is levitating. That is not a party effect." At 62×, Worker 2 has appeared by the side gate in a hazmat suit, holding a fire extinguisher pointed at the grass, gender-reveal-party style: "Extinguisher's for the vibes, ma'am." It is never used.
- **130×:** The sky over the fence turns the color of a red candle. A spotter plane circles. Sheila: "Update: the reveal has been moved up due to atmospheric conditions."
- **Overtime:** past 130× a cycle of sixteen more lines comes due every 1.35× of multiplier, forever, in order: Kyle's liquidity, Sheila's programme item forty-one ("we hold. We ALL hold."), AUNT CAROL's "?????", Tanner still blessed, the grandmothers waiting each other out, COUSIN BRAD reloading, Dale's "STILL A DOG.", Destiny asking what exactly he is a doctor of, Hal's sleeping thoughts, Tanner declining his boss three times, and Sheila: "Attendance remains perfect. Nobody leaves before the reveal."

## Suspense layer

Tension is `1 − 1/x` on the displayed multiplier (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×), so it builds through the 1×–3× window where most rounds end, with a slower log driver keeping long rounds changing. All suspense is keyed to the displayed multiplier and elapsed time, never to the outcome.

- **The cake front-runs.** The frosting is a moving canvas gradient: neutral at rest, then from about 1.7× the dye separates and the boundary sloshes like a chart, pink and blue trading places faster with the tension, with marbled swirls where they meet. At high tension both colors sweat beads that run down the tiers. A **NEUTRALITY** readout in the corner counts it down.
- **The piñata's morse** spells nothing readable but is keyed to the round's age, not its level: it ticks faster as the round gets older, a clock the party can hear and does not look at. Each tick kicks the pendulum, blinks a red light nobody admits to seeing and floats a little "tick" off it; past 13× the ticks come in dots and dashes.
- **The group chat** on the garage door scrolls faster with the multiplier and the messages degrade: "so excited" → "wait" → "sheila what is this" → "?" ×47. The message count in its header climbs.
- **The bounce house**, never inflated, lies in the sun like a fallen elephant, the subwoofer inside thumping the vinyl harder with the tension. Worker 2, in a hard hat, surfaces behind it more often as tension rises.
- **Grandma vs grandma** holds at the dessert table from 3.3× to the end: neither moves, both grip, the plate drifting a hair between them.
- **The box latch** is warm from 5×. The box is silent. The box is never opened by anyone except at the crash.
- A quiet heartbeat under the party playlist quickens with tension until the player cashes out.

## Accepted cash-out

The shell confirms the exit; only a confirmed `cashoutX100` triggers this.

Your guest — the plus-one nobody can place, seated nearest the side gate, a beanie and a mustard hoodie seen from behind with a **+1 YOU** tag — thinks "last slice. napkin. gate.", stands, takes the last slice of cake off the plate during the distraction (from between the grandmothers' hands if they have arrived), wraps it in a napkin shaped like a burrito, and leaves through the side gate past the bouncy castle that was never inflated. The gate clicks shut behind them. The badge reads **CAKE SECURED**.

Then a picture-in-picture, **BEYOND THE SIDE GATE**, shows the plus-one on the sidewalk, smug, chewing. If the chart keeps going without you, the caption keeps score: **YOU MISSED EVERYTHING. CAKE WAS GOOD THOUGH.** The party continues without the plus-one: Sheila's clipboard reads "attendance: pending", the readout says ATTENDANCE: PENDING, and the party asks after the empty chair ("Who took the last slice?", "did someone just leave with CAKE", "Whose plus-one was that?", "We should have left with the cake.", "Strike the plus-one from the group chat."). A crash after a cash-out reads as dodged: **RUG DODGED — GATE CLOSED BEHIND YOU.**

## The crash

Keyed to the crash clock, deterministic from the crash point so a replay detonates identically. The first beat lands on a hit-stop with a flash, a shake and the camera punched in on the box. Beats one to four land inside two seconds, every beat and its line by five, and the final image is complete by five and a half seconds of crash.

1. The latch. The box opens: the lid blows off and lands upside down on the lawn, the front panel falls flat. Inside: no balloon, no gender. A phone with a pre-launched token page — **$BABY IS LIVE** — propped on a fog machine that was supposed to be atmospheric and vents all at once. Sheila: "IT'S A— …it's a phone?"
2. The piñata answers it. The piñata was never a piñata: it is the dev's sell button in a papier-mâché shell, and it detonates, leaving a red **SELL** button swinging on the rope.
3. Red smoke (theatrical fog, no fire), and confetti that is actually receipts — itemized, for the crowd — fluttering down onto the folding chairs, where everyone has their phones out after all.
4. The cake collapses into its two dyes, a pink puddle and a blue one. Grandpa Hal wakes with the key finally free and uses it to open his own flask ("Finally. The key fits something."); if the round ended before he was handed it, he only wakes ("What'd I miss? Is it a boy?").
5. The banner falls and reads only **WAGMI**, the E hanging by one pin.
6. Kyle is already over the back fence with the laptop bag: "Thanks for the liquidity, babes!"
7. Destiny reads the receipt ("It's itemized. The receipt is itemized."). Destiny looks at Tanner. Tanner looks at Kyle's abandoned stool. Tanner: "We are so blessed."
8. The projected chart flatlines to zero; the garage door's screensaver is the "baby photo": a stock-photo placeholder, watermark and all, with nobody in it.

Uncle Dale, if he is up, calls it ("DOG. CALLED IT. ALWAYS A DOG."), and Sheila closes the programme: "Programme complete. Please take a receipt on your way out." Live, the next betting window opens about two seconds after the crash, so a crash the scene saw plays its ending out as an encore over the start of betting (at most three and a half seconds, keeping the crash caption, stamp and multiplier, with the music held on the crash), and only then does a short blink to black cover the yard resetting for the next round; if the next round starts running first, the blink comes at once. A crash after a cash-out is the same detonation heard small from beyond the side gate, cake in hand: the stinger is a pop, and the picture-in-picture shows a puff of red fog and a few receipts drifting over the fence while the plus-one chews (if the crash catches them still on the lawn, they hurry through the gate first).

## Sound

- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here) in its eurodance style: the party playlist, tightening with the multiplier, while the collapsed bounce house thumps to it on screen. The crash stinger is a boom.
- Cues: megaphone feedback on Sheila's programme items (`whistle`), a pop for every bubble (a bubble sound for thoughts), the piñata tick (`tick`), group-chat notifications stacking (`notify`), the key (`click`), the grandmothers arriving (`stomp`), the projector's refocus (`zap`), the boss calling (`phone`), the box latch (`creak`), "live in five" (`beep`), the spill (`splash` and a `gasp`), the levitation (`hiss`), the extinguisher (`clang`), the spotter plane (`engine`), party horns on milestones that go increasingly de-tuned (`milestone`), the heartbeat until the exit, and on cash-out the register, the slice into the napkin (`whoosh`), the side-gate latch (`click`) and a satisfied cake munch (`pop`). The crash: one bass drop (`boom`), a single long megaphone feedback (`whistle`), the piñata going off (`boom`, `pop`), a paper flurry (`whoosh`), the cake (`splash`), the banner (`thud`) and the flatline (`beep`), all of it small after a cash-out.

## Lines not to cross

- Every guest, grandmother, couple member and the dev is a generic cartoon with no likeness to any real person.
- Comedy only: the smoke is theatrical fog, the extinguisher is never discharged (least of all at a person), no fire, no gore.
- The joke mocks the over-produced party genre and the token launch, never pregnancy, parents or children. No child appears on screen: the "baby photo" is an empty stock-photo placeholder.
- No real brand of cake, projector or rideshare.
- Credits have no monetary value. Nothing in the game implies the player's own money is at stake, and the label names no opponent.

## How it is built

- `scene.ts`: composition in depth order (the sky and the backdrop, Dale over the fence, the banner, the projection, the canopy and lanterns, the gate, Worker 2 and the bounce house, the fog and the fallen receipts, Hal, Sheila, Kyle's stool, Tanner, Destiny, the box, the dessert table, Kyle under it, the grandmothers, the piñata, the walking plus-one, Kyle's jump, the folding chairs, the receipts in the air, the flash), the tension mapping, the prop beats (staged when their line is said, or once the multiplier is well past them, and settled from the multiplier on a late entry), the bubble layout (each new bubble tries a ring of places near its speaker and keeps the one that covers the fewest faces, props and earlier bubbles, inside the frame and under the HUD), the round-phase logic, the exit and the regret lines, the crash clock and its lines, the encore into betting, the blink to black, the cues and the HUD (caption, badge, multiplier, the NEUTRALITY and ATTENDANCE readouts, the stamp) and the picture-in-picture.
- `yard.ts`: the set and its props: the backdrop (sky, sun, the neighbours' roofs, the cedar fence, the garage and its panelled door, the lawn and the oak's trunk), the canopy, the lanterns, the banner (lettering painted once and cut into skewed strips so it sags in one piece, then torn into its falling pieces, the hanging E and WAGMI), the piñata pendulum and its ticks, the projection (the chart from a bounded history, the group chat, the stock-photo screensaver), the side gate, the bounce house, the dessert table with the projector, the water, the gradient cake, the last slice and the napkins, the box and its latch, and the crash's seeded receipts, piñata shards and fog puffs, each placed in closed form from the crash clock.
- `cast.ts`: the people: shared faces (eyes that look and blink on their own clocks, brows, mouths that talk), two-bone IK arms, painted-once bodies that breathe; Sheila with the clipboard, walkie and megaphone; Tanner with the mic and the boss's call; Destiny with her sash, gestures and the receipt; Hal in his lawn chair with the key, asleep, then at his flask; the grandmothers' mirrored profile rigs with a distance-driven walk in; Kyle under the cloth, peeking, on the stool, and over the fence; Dale; Worker 2 in both outfits; the folding chairs from behind; and the plus-one seated, walking (a distance-driven gait), reaching for the slice and outside the gate.
- `lines.ts`: the words and the timings: the programme, the overtime cycle, the betting and exit lines, the regret ladder, the crash's lines and its choreography clock, the captions, the stamps, the beat thresholds, the chat's senders and stages, the palette and the milestone rungs.
- `script.ts`: the dialogue engine (the ladder queue, the hold and the beat between lines, the caption, the overtime cycle, settling a late entry). `ink.ts`: the outline, the meme lettering, wrapping, outlined shapes, the speech bubbles and the cached layers. `motion.ts`: springs, easing, deterministic noise and a seeded generator. `kinematics.ts`: two-bone IK and the distance-driven gait. `portrait.ts`: the narrow-screen composition.
- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here); `pageAudio({ style: 'eurodance', crash: 'boom' })` with the cue mapping above.
- `main.ts`: the shared page shell. `replay.json`: a verified 9.07× round for the gallery, copied from the Hello World template with this game's ID.

The programme, the prop beats, the captions, the cake, the chart, the chat's pace and the HUD follow the displayed multiplier; the piñata's tick follows the round's age; the regret lines follow the multiplier against the accepted exit; nothing drawn here reads or changes the committed outcome. The crash pieces are seeded from the crash point after the crash and placed in closed form from the crash clock, so a replay throws them the same way at any frame rate, and a crash met late settles straight into its aftermath with its last line up and no cues. A scene that opens mid-round settles into place from the multiplier: the key in Hal's fist, Kyle out or on his stool, the grandmothers at the plate, the banner sagging, the latch warm, the box levitating, Dale on the fence, Worker 2 in the hazmat suit or the sky red, as far as the round has gone, with a chart already drawn and the chat already posting; an exit confirmed before the scene saw it shows the plus-one already gone. Long rounds stay bounded (at most 7 chat messages, 160 chart samples, 6 tick marks and 5 bubbles live; the crash has a fixed set of pieces) and keep changing through the overtime cycle, the chat, the morse, the plane and the sky. The crash runs on a scene-owned crash clock that follows the shell's crash age while the round is crashed and keeps running through the encore, so the ending, its lines, captions, stamp and cues complete even when the crashed phase is short; a crash met late or a scene opened in betting never encores. The cut from a crashed round to the next betting window goes through a short blink to black.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents a close-up of Sheila, the couple and the box (the plus-one's walk during the exit, the whole yard after the crash), a small overview of the backyard, the NEUTRALITY readout and the current line from the actual dialogue. The canonical shell still owns controls, round state and accepted cash-outs.
