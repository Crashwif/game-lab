# Gender Reveal

A backyard at two in the afternoon, the good kind of hot. Paper lanterns, a folding-chair semicircle of both families, a dessert table with a two-tier cake dyed questionably neutral. A banner over the fence: **BOY OR GIRL — EITHER WAY, WAGMI**. At center, a couple: **Tanner** (jorts, sunglasses on a lanyard) and **Destiny** (sash: MAMA TO BE), plus Destiny's mother **Sheila** running the event like a general, with a clipboard and a walkie-talkie. A question-mark piñata hangs from the oak. The slideshow projector someone brought is pointed at the garage door. The slideshow is the chart.

The twist, held back for one line of the programme: this is a token launch disguised as a reveal party. The "baby" is a token, and the reveal is never a baby. The couple's midwife — or "dev" — is **Kyle**, a guy in scrubs checking a laptop under the dessert table. At the center of the party, a white balloon box with a latch. Kyle has the key. The key has a keychain that says **DEV**.

The multiplier is the crowd's certainty. Every beat is keyed to the displayed multiplier, never to the crash point, and the caption is the last captioned line said, so it never runs ahead of the dialogue.

## Direction

The comedy is the collision of a genre (the gender reveal: its over-production, its violence, its certainty) with the only community that out-produces and out-uncertains it. The reveal box never opens until the crash, and the piñata was never a piñata. Keep every guest drawn as a generic cartoon; no real person's likeness, no insignia, no fire or gore — the smoke is theatrical fog, comedy only.

- Sheila speaks from the clipboard, megaphone up, everything a procedure.
- Tanner and Destiny are a negotiation. Their lines are couple-speak; their eyes are the market.
- Kyle is the dev. He is calm because he is working.
- The piñata ticks. Nobody claims it.

## Dialogue ladder

Keyed to the displayed multiplier. Each line is a speech bubble and a cue, queued so a bubble stays up at least 2.6 seconds before the same speaker's next line replaces it.

- **Betting:** Sheila, megaphone: "Phones away. Phones AWAY. If it leaks before the reveal, I will end this family." (Leak means pre-launch announcement.)
- **1.2×:** Tanner taps the mic: "Is it on? Okay. We are so blessed. We are SO blessed." Destiny's smile is a negotiation.
- **1.5×:** Grandpa Hal, military vice grip, veteran cap from a war he may have fought in the parking lot, is handed the box key "for the honor." He refuses to give it back. First omen.
- **1.8×:** The cake's frosting starts sweating; the neutral dye breaks down into pink on one side and blue on the other. The cake is front-running the reveal.
- **2.2×:** Kyle checks under the table. Destiny catches him. Kyle: "Traction's looking great, babes." On the garage door, the family group chat scrolls faster: AUNT CAROL: ??? COUSIN BRAD (from the crypto side of the family): loaded.
- **2.7×:** The piñata starts ticking. Nobody hung a ticking piñata. Sheila asks whose piñata that is. Nobody claims it.
- **3.3×:** Grandma Pauline (Destiny's side) and Grandma Bev (Tanner's side) arrive at the dessert table from opposite ends. There is one last piece of cake. They lock eyes. Stalemate.
- **4×:** The projector auto-focuses: the chart is now bigger than the banner. The banner sags. "EITHER WAY" is unpinnable.
- **5×:** Tanner's phone rings — his boss — and he declines it without looking. That is when Sheila knows. She checks the box. The latch is warm.
- **8×:** Kyle has stopped pretending. He is on a stool behind the dessert table, headset on, whispering "We're live in five. Keep the confetti warm," to someone named Worker 2.
- **13×:** Grandpa Hal has fallen asleep holding the key. The piñata is ticking in morse. Uncle Dale (a thanksgiving-uncle cameo, different shirt) yells from the fence line: "IT'S A DOG. IT'S ALWAYS A DOG." He is right. He is always right.
- **25×:** Destiny's water — a bottle of sparkling water — goes over on the dessert table. The crowd gasps at the wrong thing.
- **60×:** The balloon box starts levitating slightly. That is not a party effect. Worker 2, in a hazmat suit, has appeared by the fence holding a fire extinguisher, gender-reveal-party style.
- **130×:** The sky over the fence turns the color of a red candle. A spotter plane circles. The reveal has been moved up due to atmospheric conditions.

The couple gets a word in between lines, and so does the chat: Destiny, not to Tanner, "Did you invite the scrubs guy?" Tanner, not to Destiny, "We don't know him, he came with the bounce house." AUNT CAROL: "why is there a chart on the garage."

## Suspense layer

Tension is `1 − 1/x` on the displayed multiplier (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×), so it builds through the 1×–3× window where most rounds end, with a slower log driver keeping long rounds changing. All suspense is keyed to the displayed multiplier and elapsed time, never to the outcome.

- **The cake front-runs.** The dye separation is a chart: pink and blue trade places with tension. At high tension both colors sweat.
- **The piñata's morse** spells nothing readable but is keyed to the multiplier's rate, not its level: it ticks faster as the round gets older, a clock the party can hear and does not look at.
- **The group chat** scrolls faster with the multiplier; the messages degrade: "so excited" → "wait" → "sheila what is this" → "?" ×47.
- **The bounce house**, never inflated, lies in the sun like a fallen elephant. Worker 2 appears beside it more often as tension rises.
- **Grandma vs grandma** holds at the dessert table the whole round: neither moves, both grip.
- **The box latch** is warm from 5×. The box is silent. The box is never opened by anyone except at the crash.
- A quiet heartbeat under the party playlist quickens with tension until the player cashes out.

## Accepted cash-out

The shell confirms the exit; only a confirmed `cashoutX100` triggers this.

Your guest — the plus-one nobody can place, seated nearest the side gate — takes the last slice of cake during the distraction, wraps it in a napkin shaped like a burrito, and leaves through the side gate past the bouncy castle that was never inflated. The badge reads **CAKE SECURED**.

If the chart keeps going without you, the caption keeps score: **YOU MISSED EVERYTHING. CAKE WAS GOOD THOUGH.** The party continues without the plus-one, Sheila's clipboard reads "attendance: pending." A crash after a cash-out reads as dodged: **RUG DODGED — GATE CLOSED BEHIND YOU.**

## The crash

Keyed to `view.crashAge`, deterministic from the crash point so a replay detonates identically.

1. The latch. The box opens. Inside: no balloon, no gender. A phone with a pre-launched token page — **$BABY IS LIVE** — and a fog machine that was supposed to be atmospheric vents all at once.
2. The piñata answers it. The piñata was never a piñata: it is the dev's sell button in a papier-mâché shell, and it detonates.
3. Red smoke (theatrical fog, no fire), and confetti that is actually receipts — itemized, itemized for the crowd.
4. The cake collapses into its two dyes. Grandpa Hal wakes with the key finally free and uses it to open his own flask.
5. The banner falls and reads only **WAGMI**, the E hanging by one pin.
6. Kyle is already over the back fence with the laptop bag.
7. Destiny reads the receipt. Destiny looks at Tanner. Tanner looks at Kyle's abandoned stool. Tanner: "We are so blessed."
8. The projected chart flatlines to zero; the garage door's screensaver is a baby photo — a stock photo, watermark and all.

A short blink to black covers the yard resetting for the next round. A crash after a cash-out is the same detonation heard small from beyond the side gate, cake in hand.

## Sound

- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here). Party playlist with a subwoofer hidden in the collapsed bounce house, tightening with the multiplier.
- Cues: megaphone feedback at programme beats (`fx`), the piñata tick (`tick`), group-chat notifications stacking (`notify`), the box latch (`creak`), party horns on milestones that go increasingly de-tuned (`milestone`), the side-gate latch plus a satisfied cake munch on cash-out, and the crash: one bass drop, a paper flurry, and a single long megaphone feedback.

## Lines not to cross

- Every guest, grandmother, couple member and the dev is a generic cartoon with no likeness to any real person.
- Comedy only: the smoke is theatrical fog, the extinguisher is never discharged at a person, no fire, no gore.
- The joke mocks the over-produced party genre and the token launch, never pregnancy, parents or children. No child appears on screen.
- No real brand of cake, projector or rideshare.
- Credits have no monetary value. Nothing in the game implies the player's own money is at stake, and the label names no opponent.

## Builder's notes

This skeleton copies the [Hello World](../hello-world) template verbatim: `main.ts`, `audio.ts` and the shared CSS block are unmodified shared-shell copies; `scene.ts` is the commented template with its circle and greeting, fully functional for every phase. The build, replay fixture (`replay.json`'s `gameId` names this slug) and recorded example are already wired.

To build the game on this skeleton, work in `scene.ts` against the `SceneView` contract documented in the template's [README](../hello-world/README.md#where-to-work): render `waiting`, `betting`, `running` and `crashed` from `view.phase`, use `view.currentX100` for the multiplier (150 means 1.50×), `view.elapsed` for running animation and `view.crashAge` for the ending. Pacing matters more than anything else: about half of all rounds end before 2× (9 seconds in) and two in three before 3×, so the early dialogue beats arrive every few seconds through 3× and a slower `log10` driver keeps long rounds changing. Tension is presentation only — never derive it from the crash point. Every animation is a pure function of the view, so replays, seeks and late entries draw the same frame; cues fire on confirmed changes only, and a late entry shows the current state without replaying an old cash-out or crash. The crash keys off `view.crashAge`. Keep `main.ts`, `audio.ts` and the shared CSS block untouched; shared changes belong in [`scripts/shell/`](../../scripts/shell/).

Read [the animation reference](../../docs/animation.md) before adding or changing character animation: the dye separation is a canvas-gradient trick that reads as fluid, the two grandmothers' stalemate is a profile-pair rig with mirrored tension, and the piñata is a pendulum rig on a spring with its own tick. Check waiting, running, crash, accepted cash-out, replay and small-screen behavior when relevant, then run `npm run build`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run check` and `npm test`.
