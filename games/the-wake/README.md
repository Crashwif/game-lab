# The Wake

A funeral home parlor at half past four in the afternoon. Late light through venetian blinds, too many lilies, a guest book on a stand by the door titled **HOLDERS**. Center: an open casket. Gerald "Big Bagz" Holdern — a fictional, generic Wojak-adjacent cartoon, no likeness to anyone — died doing what he loved: not selling. In his folded hands, his phone, screen still on, lock screen showing the chart, battery at 4%. His wrist wears a hardware wallet on a rosary chain. Above the casket, his LinkedIn photo with a red laser-eye filter, the kind that was popular briefly and ironically. Wreaths shaped like candle charts flank the casket; their ribbons read DIAMOND HANDS and WEN RESURRECTION.

The mourners, front to back:

- **The widow**, black veil, front row, clutching a folded lawn chair — Gerald's stadium seat, the one thing the creditor missed.
- **Her mother**, beside her, with the guest book's spare pen and an opinion about lilies.
- **The teenage son**, hoodie, running a chart overlay on his phone over the eulogy.
- **The best friend and financial advisor**, back row, quietly on his phone. He sold at 1.4×. He is the one giving the eulogy's closing, and he does not know he is the villain.
- **The dog**, flat on the floor by the casket, ears moving.
- **Your mourner**, a cousin near the back, seen from behind. This is the player: the cash-out is leaving the service.

The officiant is a hired **Crypto Celebrant**: bow tie, ledger-shaped prayer book, a stole with the ticker woven in. His eulogy is the game's spine.

The multiplier is how deep the eulogy goes. Every line is keyed to the displayed multiplier, never to the crash point, and the caption is the last line said, so it never runs ahead of the dialogue.

## Direction

The comedy is tonal whiplash between the solemnity of the form (eulogy, prayer, guest book, casket) and the language of the portfolio (bags, liquidity, rotation, entry). Nothing is funnier or sadder than the same sentence being a prayer and a position. Keep every mourner drawn as a generic cartoon; no real person's likeness, no insignia, no gore, comedy only.

- The celebrant speaks from the curve, hands rising off the book as tension rises.
- The widow reacts with physics, not words. Her only line is at the crash.
- The son's phone overlay tracks the chart; the best friend's phone tracks his exit.
- The phone in the casket glows on the lock screen and never unlocks. Its battery drains as the round runs — a countdown nobody in the room can see.

## Dialogue ladder

Keyed to the displayed multiplier. Each line is a speech bubble above the celebrant and a cue.

- **Betting:** "We are gathered here today because Gerald refused to take profits. In life, and now in portfolio."
- **1.3×:** "Gerald believed. When the chart went down, he called it a discount. When it went up, he called it validation. When it did nothing for nine months, he called it patience."
- **1.7×:** "He is survived by his wife, his dog, and 4.2 billion $BAGS, currently in a locked wallet whose password died with him."
- **2.1×:** "He asked that, in lieu of flowers, you airdrop something to the memorial wallet."
- **2.6×:** "His last words, typed at 3:51 am, were: 'This is a dip. A dip to the underworld, but a dip.'"
- **3.2×:** "He wrote his own eulogy. It is one page. Three hundred words. Two hundred and ninety of them are 'average in'."
- **3.8×:** "This service is paid for — not by the estate, but by the community wallet. Which also paid for this microphone, these flowers, and me. We are all liquidity now."
- **5×:** "Some men leave a house. Some leave a car. Gerald leaves a chart, and on that chart a message for the generations: he never sold."
- **8×:** "We do not say 'lost'. We say 'early'. Gerald was early for everything, and this funeral is also early —"
- **12×:** "— the hearse has been repossessed. We will carry him to the grave ourselves, in shifts, like holders."
- **20×:** "His candle burns eternal. Not the votive: the one on the chart behind me, which the gravediggers are at this hour also checking."
- **40×:** "Dearly beloved, we are gathered today inside Gerald's average entry, and it is beautiful."
- **100×:** "Gerald did not die. He rotated into a better chain."

The family gets a word in between lines, queued so a bubble stays up at least 2.6 seconds before the same speaker's next line replaces it: the son, not looking up, "Dad said no open casket, he said show the bags." The mother, dabbing with the guest book's page: "He was a good boy." The best friend, into his phone, "I'll call you back."

## Suspense layer

Tension is `1 − 1/x` on the displayed multiplier (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×), so it builds through the 1×–3× window where most rounds end, with a slower log driver keeping long rounds changing. All suspense is keyed to the displayed multiplier and elapsed time, never to the outcome.

- **Mourner phone checks.** As tension rises, mourners one by one glance at their own phones under their jacket linings, in sequence seeded from the round. Nobody checks a phone before 1.5×. By 5× the whole back row glows.
- **The guest book turns its own pages.** Wind, or something. It flips forward with tension.
- **The florist** at the back of the room is running a stop-loss on the wreaths; at high tension she is typing.
- **The gravediggers** outside the window pause, lean on shovels, and watch the parlor's glass.
- **A leaf on the nearest wreath** curls at 5× — the first visible wilt, foreshadowing the crash.
- **The casket phone's battery** drains with the multiplier: 4% at 1×, 1% at 10×. Nobody in the room can see it. The player can.
- **The dog** looks from the casket to the flowers and back, timing something.

A quiet heartbeat under the organ music quickens with tension until the player cashes out.

## Accepted cash-out

The shell confirms the exit; only a confirmed `cashoutX100` triggers this.

Your mourner slips out the side door mid-eulogy: shades on, past the reception table, two sandwiches and the commemorative candle taken, into a waiting rideshare whose plate reads **TOOK PROFIT**. The badge reads **SOLD BEFORE THE SERVICE**.

If the chart keeps going without you, the caption keeps score: **THE WAKE GOES ON. HE RESTS. YOU EAT.** The service continues without the cousin, the celebrant accelerates, the family endures. A crash after a cash-out reads as dodged: **RUG DODGED — CANDLE STILL WARM.**

## The crash

Keyed to `view.crashAge`, deterministic from the crash point so a replay tumbles identically.

1. The phone in the casket's hands buzzes once. **PUSH NOTIFICATION: DEV SOLD.** Liquidity gone.
2. The portrait's laser eyes go dark — the laser-eye filter was the only thing keeping the photo alive.
3. Every wreath ribbon unrolls in seeded order to read **RUGGED**; the chart-shaped wreath wilts leaf by leaf.
4. The celebrant closes his ledger-book, which is empty. It was always empty.
5. The widow stands, unfolds the lawn chair, and sits at the foot of the casket facing the mourners. Her only line: **"I told him about the mortgage."**
6. The guest book's last page turns by itself to a blank page that reads **WEN WAKE #2**.
7. In the driveway window, the hearse drives off without its cargo.
8. The dog's final head tilt is the last movement on screen before the fade.

A short blink to black covers the parlor resetting for the next round. A crash after a cash-out is the same crash played small behind the departing cousin's shoulder.

## Post-crash coda

The reception table, visible at the frame's edge: a sheet cake reading **HODL** in icing, uneaten.

## Sound

- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here). A lo-fi organ that opens up with the multiplier.
- Cues: eulogy lines as page-flip and organ chord, a soft bell toll on milestones (`milestone`), the mourner phone-checks as single quiet ticks (`tick`), the casket phone's buzz (`notify`), the widow's veil tremble as soft fabric, the dog's ears (nothing), the rideshare horn on cash-out, and the crash: one buzz, then silence, then the lawn chair unfolding.

## Lines not to cross

- Gerald is a generic cartoon with no likeness to any real person.
- Comedy only: the casket stays closed to the audience, nothing in it is ever shown beyond the phone screen and folded hands. No gore, no body horror.
- Grief is the widow's to hold, and she is never the punchline; the portfolio is. Her one line lands because it is true, not because she is mocked.
- No religious rite is performed on screen beyond the celebrant's invented Crypto Celebrant patter, which belongs to no actual faith.
- Credits have no monetary value. Nothing in the game implies the player's own money is at stake, and the label names no opponent.

## Builder's notes

This skeleton copies the [Hello World](../hello-world) template verbatim: `main.ts`, `audio.ts` and the shared CSS block are unmodified shared-shell copies; `scene.ts` is the commented template with its circle and greeting, fully functional for every phase. The build, replay fixture (`replay.json`'s `gameId` names this slug) and recorded example are already wired.

To build the game on this skeleton, work in `scene.ts` against the `SceneView` contract documented in the template's [README](../hello-world/README.md#where-to-work): render `waiting`, `betting`, `running` and `crashed` from `view.phase`, use `view.currentX100` for the multiplier (150 means 1.50×), `view.elapsed` for running animation and `view.crashAge` for the ending. Pacing matters more than anything else: about half of all rounds end before 2× (9 seconds in) and two in three before 3×, so the early dialogue beats arrive every few seconds through 3× and a slower `log10` driver keeps long rounds changing. Tension is presentation only — never derive it from the crash point. Every animation is a pure function of the view, so replays, seeks and late entries draw the same frame; cues fire on confirmed changes only, and a late entry shows the current state without replaying an old cash-out or crash. The crash keys off `view.crashAge`. Keep `main.ts`, `audio.ts` and the shared CSS block untouched; shared changes belong in [`scripts/shell/`](../../scripts/shell/).

Read [the animation reference](../../docs/animation.md) before adding or changing character animation: the widow's veil and the mourners' reactions are secondary motion on springs, the celebrant's hands are a two-bone rig with lag, and the wreath wilt is a seeded per-leaf deformation. Check waiting, running, crash, accepted cash-out, replay and small-screen behavior when relevant, then run `npm run build`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run check` and `npm test`.
