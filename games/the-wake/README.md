# The Wake

A funeral home parlor at half past four in the afternoon. Plum damask walls, late light through venetian blinds laid in stripes across the room with dust turning in it, too many lilies, a clock stopped near 4:30, and by the side door a guest book on a stand whose brass plate reads **HOLDERS**. Center: an open casket on a draped bier. Gerald "Big Bagz" Holdern — a fictional, generic Wojak-adjacent cartoon, no likeness to anyone — died doing what he loved: not selling. The casket's side panel hides him; all that shows over the rim are his folded hands around his phone, screen still on, lock screen showing the chart, battery at 4%, and from his wrist a hardware wallet hanging on a bead chain over the side. Above the casket, his networking-site headshot with a red laser-eye filter, the kind that was popular briefly and ironically. Two wreaths on easels flank the casket, rings of leaves with chart candles burning inside them; their ribbons read DIAMOND HANDS and WEN RESURRECTION. Through the window: the cemetery lawn, two gravediggers at a fresh grave and the hearse in the driveway. At the frame's left edge, the reception table and its sheet cake.

The mourners, on folding chairs, front to back:

- **The widow**, black hat and veil, front row on the aisle, gripping a folded lawn chair that stands beside her — Gerald's stadium seat, the one thing the creditor missed.
- **Her mother**, beside her, with a page torn from the guest book and an opinion about lilies.
- **The teenage son**, hoodie, running a chart overlay on his phone over the eulogy; you can read it over his shoulder.
- **The best friend and financial advisor**, back row, quietly on his phone, which says SOLD 1.40×. He sold at 1.4×. He is the one giving the eulogy's closing, and he does not know he is the villain.
- **The dog**, flat on the carpet by the casket, ears moving.
- **The florist**, standing at the back of the room with a tablet.
- **Your mourner**, a cousin standing near the back by the side door, seen from behind under a YOU tag. This is the player: the cash-out is leaving the service.

The officiant is a hired **Crypto Celebrant** at a lectern beside the casket: bow tie, a green ledger for a prayer book, a stole with the ticker ($BAGS) woven in, a microphone the community wallet paid for. His eulogy is the game's spine.

The multiplier is how deep the eulogy goes. Every line is keyed to the displayed multiplier, never to the crash point, and the caption is the last line said, so it never runs ahead of the dialogue.

## Direction

The comedy is tonal whiplash between the solemnity of the form (eulogy, guest book, casket) and the language of the portfolio (bags, liquidity, rotation, entry). Nothing is funnier or sadder than the same sentence being a prayer and a position. Every mourner is drawn as a generic cartoon; no real person's likeness, no insignia, no gore, comedy only.

- The celebrant speaks from the curve, hands rising off the book as tension rises: on the ledger at rest, gesturing at chest height by 2×, arms spread high past 5×; he gestures faster once the cousin has gone.
- The widow reacts with physics, not words: her veil trembles on a spring that every line and every milestone kicks, her shoulders rise, and her knuckles whiten on the lawn chair's top bar. Her only line is at the crash.
- The son's phone overlay tracks the chart (the round's curve to the displayed multiplier); the best friend's phone shows his exit, and goes to his ear when he speaks.
- The phone in the casket glows on the lock screen and never unlocks. Its battery drains as the round runs — a countdown nobody in the room can see. The HUD shows it: HIS PHONE 4%.

## Dialogue ladder

Keyed to the displayed multiplier. The celebrant's lines are speech bubbles beside him with a page-turn cue; the family's are bubbles by whoever says them, queued so a bubble stays up at least 2.6 seconds before the same speaker's next line replaces it and a beat passes between lines.

- **Betting:** "We are gathered here today because Gerald refused to take profits. In life, and now in portfolio."
- **1.02×:** "Let us begin. Phones on silent. Charts face down, please." (the mother, 1.12×: "Lilies. Of course it's lilies.")
- **1.3×:** "Gerald believed. When it went down, he called it a discount. When it went up, validation. When it did nothing for nine months: patience." (the son, 1.42×, not looking up: "Dad said no open casket. He said show the bags.")
- **1.7×:** "He is survived by his wife, his dog, and 4.2 billion $BAGS, in a locked wallet whose password died with him." (the mother, 1.86×, dabbing with the guest book's page: "He was a good boy.")
- **2.1×:** "He asked that, in lieu of flowers, you airdrop something to the memorial wallet." A MEMORIAL WALLET placard appears against the bier. (The best friend, 2.3×, into his phone: "I'll call you back.")
- **2.6×:** "His last words, typed at 3:51 am, were: 'This is a dip. A dip to the underworld, but a dip.'" (the son, 2.85×: "Chart's still up. He would be so annoying about this.")
- **3.2×:** "He wrote his own eulogy. One page. Three hundred words. Two hundred and ninety of them are 'average in'." (the mother, 3.5×: "Tulips would have been nicer. Tulips also crash.")
- **3.8×:** "This service is paid for by the community wallet. So were the flowers, the microphone, and me. We are all liquidity now." (the friend, 4.3×: "No, I'm at a thing. Yes, still out. Since 1.4.")
- **5×:** "Some men leave a house. Some leave a car. Gerald leaves a chart, and on it a message for the generations: he never sold." (the son, 6.2×: "Overlay says he's up. He's still dead, though.")
- **8×:** "We do not say 'lost'. We say 'early'. Gerald was early for everything, and this funeral is also early —" (the mother, 10×: "I signed the guest book twice. For the average.")
- **12×:** "— the hearse has been repossessed. We will carry him to the grave ourselves, in shifts, like holders." A REPO notice appears on the hearse in the driveway. (The friend, 15×: "Tell them I'm doing the closing. Yes. The eulogy closing.")
- **20×:** "His candle burns eternal. Not the votive: the one on the chart behind me, which the gravediggers are also checking." The gravediggers' phones light up. (The son, 28×: "Can we bury the phone with him? It's still pumping.")
- **40×:** "Dearly beloved, we are gathered today inside Gerald's average entry, and it is beautiful." (The mother, 60×: "I said tulips. Nobody listens.")
- **100×:** "Gerald did not die. He rotated into a better chain."

Past 100× a twelve-line overtime cycle comes due every 1.35× of multiplier for as long as the round runs: "He is simply staked now, for an indefinite lock-up period.", "Let us bow our heads, and check our phones.", the best friend's closing ("In closing: Gerald, I sold at 1.4. For both of us."), "The gravediggers have stopped digging. They are holding.", "We will now observe a moment of volatility.", "His average entry is now visible from space.", "The eulogy will continue until morale improves, or the chart doesn't.", "He is survived by the chart. The chart is doing great.", with the son, the mother and the friend in between.

## Suspense layer

Tension is `1 − 1/x` on the displayed multiplier (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×), so it builds through the 1×–3× window where most rounds end, with a slower log driver keeping long rounds changing. All suspense is keyed to the displayed multiplier and elapsed time, never to the outcome.

- **Mourner phone checks.** Four mourners take turns (1.55×, 2.25×, 3.05×, 4.1×, assigned in a seeded order) glancing at phones under their jacket linings: an elbow out, the head bowed and turned, the screen's light on the face, a quiet tick for each. Nobody checks a phone before 1.5×; the glances come more often as the tension rises, and from 5× the whole back row glows.
- **The guest book turns its own pages.** Wind, or something: a page lifts and lays itself over, slowly at first, faster with the tension.
- **The florist** at the back of the room is running a stop-loss on the wreaths (STOP-LOSS: WREATHS on her tablet, the price sliding toward her line); past two-thirds tension she is typing.
- **The gravediggers** outside the window dig, then from about 1.5× pause, lean on their shovels and watch the parlor's glass.
- **A leaf on the nearest wreath** curls at 5× — the first visible wilt, foreshadowing the crash.
- **The casket phone's battery** drains with the multiplier: 4% at 1×, 3% from 2.15×, 2% from 4.64×, 1% at 10×, and there it holds. Nobody in the room can see it. The player can (HIS PHONE n%).
- **The dog** looks from the casket to the flowers and back, faster as the room tightens, timing something; his ears swing on springs and twitch.

A quiet heartbeat under the organ quickens with tension until the player cashes out.

## Accepted cash-out

The shell confirms the exit; only a confirmed `cashoutX100` triggers this.

Your mourner slips out the side door mid-eulogy, thinking "sorry. my ride's here.": shades on, a turn, a few steps to the reception table where one hand takes two sandwiches and then the commemorative candle, then away into the room's depth to the side door, which swings open on the evening light. In the window a rideshare has pulled up with its hazards blinking and a horn; its plate reads **TOOK PROFIT**. The cousin crosses the driveway, gets in, and the car drives off into the distance. The badge reads **SOLD BEFORE THE SERVICE**, and the caption cycles SOLD BEFORE THE SERVICE, LEFT AT THE EULOGY, TOUCHED GRASS AT A FUNERAL.

If the chart keeps going without you, the caption keeps score from 1.5× past the exit: **THE WAKE GOES ON. HE RESTS. YOU EAT.** The service continues without the cousin, the celebrant gestures faster, the family endures, and the room says so: "Was that the cousin? With the candle?" at 1.5× past the exit, "Some of us took profits during the eulogy. Gerald forgives you. His chart does not." at 2×, "Cousin's rideshare is up more than Dad." at 3×, "Smart kid. I'll call him back." at 5×, "The empty spot at the back has outperformed the deceased." at 10×. A crash after a cash-out reads as dodged: **RUG DODGED — CANDLE STILL WARM.**

## The crash

Keyed to the crash clock (the seconds since the crash; the choreography runs half a second behind it through the hit-stop), deterministic from the crash point so a replay tumbles identically. The beats are front-loaded because the room moves on to the next betting phase about two seconds after a crash: beats 1–4 land within two seconds, the widow speaks at about 3.5 s and the final image is complete by 5.4 s (ENDING_S). A crash seen live plays on into the betting phase (an encore, at most 3.5 s, still captioned and stamped as the crash) until that ending is complete, then blinks to black into the fresh betting set.

1. The phone in the casket's hands buzzes once, the camera punched in on it after a 0.15 s freeze. **PUSH NOTIFICATION: DEV SOLD.** Liquidity gone. The card rises out of the phone and folds back into it by 2.5 s.
2. At 0.7 s the portrait's laser eyes flicker and go dark, and the photo greys — the laser-eye filter was the only thing keeping it alive.
3. From 1 s every ribbon (both wreath sashes, the casket spray's and the vase's) flips over and runs long to read **RUGGED**, a fifth of a second apart in a seeded order, all four by 2 s; the wreaths wilt leaf by leaf in a seeded order, and nearly half the leaves drop to the carpet.
4. The celebrant's hands go back to the book and close it (by 1.8 s): it was always empty, and a moth flies out. He bows his head, eyes shut.
5. At 1.7 s the widow stands, carries the lawn chair up the aisle to the foot of the casket, turns to face the room, unfolds it (the creak) and sits in it facing the mourners. Her only line, at about 3.5 s, stays up through the final image: **"I told him about the mortgage."**
6. At 3.9 s the guest book's last page turns by itself to a blank page that reads **WEN WAKE #2**.
7. At 4 s, in the driveway window, the hearse drives off without its cargo. The phone in the casket dies.
8. At 4.9 s the dog's final head tilt is the last movement on screen; from 5.3 s the final image holds.

The mourners bow their heads. From 1.4 s the stamp reads BURIED WITH THE BAGS (JUST PAYING RESPECTS for a spectator), over the empty foot of the aisle and never over a live speech bubble (LEFT WITH THE CANDLE after an exit, under the phone), and the caption cycles the crash ladder. A short blink to black covers the parlor resetting for the next round. A crash after a cash-out is the same crash played small: the camera pulls back into the rideshare, over the departing cousin's shoulder, where the wake is streaming LIVE on their phone, a bitten sandwich in one hand and the candle on the seat. An instant crash at 1.00× plays the same beats before any eulogy.

## Post-crash coda

The reception table, visible at the frame's edge: a sheet cake reading **HODL** in icing, uneaten. From 4.3 s the room dims around it until it is the only thing the late light still finds.

## Sound

- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here), as `pageAudio({ style: 'lofi', crash: 'static', bpm: 64, tempoRise: 0.3 })`: a slow lo-fi organ with vinyl crackle that opens up with the multiplier.
- Cues: each eulogy line a page-turn (`whoosh`) over the organ, the family's lines a soft `pop`, the cousin's whisper a `bubble`; a bell toll on milestones (`milestone`) with the widow's veil as soft fabric (a faint `whoosh`); the mourners' phone checks as single quiet ticks (`tick`); the guest book's pages (`whoosh`); the cash-out register (`cashout`), the rideshare horn (`beep`, twice) and the side door (`door`); the crash is one `buzz` with the `notify` and the `static` stinger, then silence, then the lawn chair unfolding (`creak`). The dog's ears make no sound.

## Lines not to cross

- Gerald is a generic cartoon with no likeness to any real person.
- Comedy only: the casket stays closed to the audience, nothing in it is ever shown beyond the phone screen and folded hands. No gore, no body horror.
- Grief is the widow's to hold, and she is never the punchline; the portfolio is. Her one line lands because it is true, not because she is mocked.
- No religious rite is performed on screen beyond the celebrant's invented Crypto Celebrant patter, which belongs to no actual faith.
- Credits have no monetary value. Nothing in the game implies the player's own money is at stake, and the label names no opponent.

## How it is built

- `scene.ts`: the composition in depth order (the backdrop, the window, the door, the portrait's filter, the vase and the wreaths, the casket, the celebrant behind his lectern and ledger, then the dog, the widow, the rows, the guest book, the florist, the reception table and the cousin sorted by where they stand, the crash props, the late light, the coda and the bubbles), the clocks (a hit-stop and slow motion on the crash, the crash clock and the choreography clock derived from it, the encore into the next betting phase, an ambient clock that stops for the final image), the round-phase logic with late entry and quiet crashes, the exit and the regret lines, the bubble placement, the cues, the HUD (caption, badge, multiplier, HIS PHONE n%, outcome stamp), the rideshare framing of a dodged crash and the portrait compositor's crops.
- `parlor.ts`: the set. The walls, carpet, window view, door casing, sconces, clock and Gerald's photograph are painted once into a cached backdrop; the wreaths, vase, basket, casket, lectern, blinds, hearse, guest book stand and reception table are cached stamps with their moving parts drawn live (candle flames, ribbons, the curl leaf and the crash's wilt, the phone and its glow, the bead chain, the sandwiches and the candle). Also the gravediggers, the hearse's REPO notice and its drive-off, the rideshare and the tiny cousin outside, the guest book's pages, the laser-eye filter, the push notification, the moth, the light and its dust, and the coda.
- `folk.ts`: the people. The celebrant facing us (a cached torso that breathes and leans, a face whose brows, eyes and mouth follow his lines, two-bone arms solved toward hand targets that rise off the ledger with the tension and come back to close it); seated mourners from behind (a cached resting pose swayed and breathed by its transform, drawn live when they turn, bow or glance at a phone); the widow's grip, knuckles, veil, walk, turn, lawn chair and seat; the mother's dab; the son's overlay; the best friend's phone; the dog's look cycle, ear springs and tilt; the florist's typing; the cousin's hunch, glances, shades, reach (two-bone, to the platter and the candle) and walk into the room's depth.
- `ride.ts`: the back seat of the rideshare for a crash after the exit: the street going by, the phone streaming the parlor, the cousin's head and hands, the sandwich and the candle.
- `lines.ts`: the eulogy ladder, the interjections, the overtime cycle, the betting line, the captions, the regret and crash lines, the stamps and the milestone rungs.
- `script.ts`: the dialogue engine: lines due on the displayed multiplier, said through a queue that holds each speaker's bubble long enough to read, the overtime cycle in closed form, settling for late entry, bounded bubbles and queue.
- `ink.ts`: the outline colour, the meme lettering, prop labels, text wrapping, outlined shapes and the speech bubbles. `motion.ts`: springs, easing, deterministic noise and a seeded generator. `kinematics.ts`: the two-bone limb solver with a continuous pole. `portrait.ts`: the narrow-screen composition.
- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here), used as described under Sound. `main.ts`: the shared page shell. `replay.json`: a verified 9.07× round for the gallery, copied from the Hello World template with this game's ID.

Every line, caption, reaction, prop beat, suspense cue and the HUD follow the displayed multiplier, the round clock or the crash clock; the regret lines follow the multiplier against the accepted exit; nothing drawn here reads or changes the committed outcome. The crash point is used only once the crash has happened, to seed the order the ribbons unroll and the leaves wilt and fall, so a replay plays the crash the same way every time. A scene that opens mid-round, after the crash or after an exit settles into place without replaying a cue: the eulogy's last lines up and the caption it reached, the placard, the REPO notice and the curled leaf where the multiplier has them, the gravediggers watching, the mourners' phones, the cousin already gone if the exit was accepted, or the aftermath at its crash-clock moment with the widow already in her chair. Long rounds keep talking through the overtime cycle and keep moving (breathing, glances, flames, the dog, the gravediggers) up to 180 s and 1,000,000×, with at most five bubbles and four queued lines alive. The cut from a crashed round to the next betting window goes through a short blink to black, after the encore when the crash was seen live. Sound is opt-in through the Sound button.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents a close-up of the casket, the celebrant and the front rows, a small overview that keeps the whole parlor, and the current line from the actual dialogue; the close-up follows the cousin out of the door on an exit, the casket and the widow at the crash, and the phone in the rideshare after a dodged crash. The canonical shell still owns controls, round state and accepted cash-outs.

Run `npm run preview -- the-wake` from the repository root and open the printed URL. Join with 50 local credits, then cash out during a running round to slip out of the side door. `npm run build` produces the three publishable files in `dist/the-wake/`.

All visuals are original Canvas 2D shapes and text; sound uses the repository's shared procedural helper (`clips.json` is `{}`). Gerald, his family, the celebrant and everyone at the wake are fictional. Credits have no monetary value. No third-party assets are included. The game is an open remix origin (`gallery.json`).
