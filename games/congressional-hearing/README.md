# Congressional Hearing

A committee room. Wood panelling, a navy backdrop with the committee's seal, two plain blue flags, a curved dais with five senators who have clearly never held a phone, and a witness table with one **hoodie** at it. The witness is seen from behind and a little to the side, and his face is never shown: the hood is a shadow, the answer is the game. A stenographer types on a paper typewriter that feeds the minutes onto a board on the wall, a sergeant at arms checks his watch, a page runs in and out with a card, the clerk's desk holds a bouquet of bail applications, and in the gallery nearest the camera the public sits in seats labelled **PUBLIC** — generic cartoons seen from behind, half of them filming vertical. Through a window cut into the left frame edge, Senator Vance's two boats ride at anchor.

The five senators, dais left to right: **Chairman Marcus** (never heard of the internet, chain of office, gavel), **Senator Vance** (financial literacy subcommittee chair, owns two boats, both titled to a Cayman entity he calls "the family office"), **Senator Ruiz** (has read one white paper, brings it up), **Senator Howe** (asleep, wakes to vote against), and **Senator Bell** (highest coins, worst questions, a hardware wallet she does not understand).

The multiplier is how long the coin holds the committee's attention, and every step up is a worse question, a longer answer, and a bigger opportunity for the gallery.

## Direction

The comedy is the gap between the machine of government (oaths, minutes, seniority, subcommittees) and the machine of the coin (one guy in a hoodie, total supply fixed at birth, liquidity gone). The senators are not evil: they are worse, they are *senatorial*. Every witness answer is technically true and completely useless. Every senator's question reveals an unreported interest. The game never names a real committee, a real party, a real country's government: the flags are two plain blue fields, the seal is a ledger with wings, the letter board on the dais reads COMMITTEE ON and then five unassigned tiles.

- The witness answers with a full sentence of nothing: "Chairman, I appreciate the question, and the short answer is yes."
- The senators speak from the dais, one at a time, seniority order, and every one of them has an angle.
- The stenographer's minutes stack on the wall as the round runs — the game's own scoreboard.
- The gallery gets bolder with the multiplier: it is the exits and the bids.

## Dialogue ladder

Keyed to the displayed multiplier. Each exchange is two bubbles (senator, witness), the witness's reply held back a beat, and a cue; a bubble stays up at least 2.6 seconds before the same speaker's next line replaces it, and a senator taking the floor retires the last senator's long bubble. The caption is the last captioned line said.

- **Betting:** Chairman Marcus, gavel: "This hearing is called to order. The committee will hear testimony on… the coin. Which is. Which one is it?" The page runs in with a card: "The coin."
- **1.15×:** Senator Vance: "So the money is in the computer. Whose computer?" Witness: "Chairman, I appreciate the question, and the short answer is yes."
- **1.35×:** Senator Ruiz, white paper up: "I've read the white paper. Page nine. Where is the page nine of this hearing?" Witness: "The white paper has been amended in spirit."
- **1.55×:** Senator Bell, wallet up: "My constituents keep sending me this wallet — a hardware wallet, it sounds like a — what is it, a drive? — is my money in it?" Witness: "No."
- **1.8×:** Chairman Marcus, gavel: "Let the record show the witness said no."
- **2.1×:** Senator Vance: "I'm going to ask the hard question. The supply is fixed at 21 million. Who sets the inflation?" Witness: "The market." Senator Vance: "I see. And who sets the market?" Witness: "The market."
- **2.4×:** Senator Ruiz: "For the record, this is the same technology as the internet, which I also use."
- **2.8×:** Senator Howe wakes, fist up: "Objection. Whatever we're doing, my state has a refinery." He goes back to sleep.
- **3.2×:** Senator Bell: "The hoodie. Is the hoodie a uniform of any organization?" Witness: "It is not." Chairman Marcus: "Let the record show the hoodie is not a uniform."
- **3.8×:** Senator Vance holds up his bill: "My subcommittee on financial literacy has drafted a bill. I have it here. It is one sentence. 'Give me some.'" The page runs to the witness's table and back.
- **4.5×:** Chairman Marcus: "We're going around the room, one line each, for what the members are calling 'a position.'" Every senator, in seniority order, a hand up: "In." "In." "In." "Refinery." "In."
- **5.5×:** Senator Ruiz: "Directly, then. Is the coin a security?" Witness: "The coin is a coin." Chairman Marcus: "Let the record show the coin is a coin."
- **7×:** Senator Bell: "One more from me. The wallet I bought — the hardware one — does it also do the phones?" Witness: "No."
- **9×:** Senator Vance, to the gallery, on the record: "My subcommittee will be accepting a limited number of new investors. This is not an offer of securities. This is a boat." The gallery applauds.
- **12×:** Chairman Marcus, to the page: "What time is the next hearing?" The page, at the dais: "There is no next hearing, Chairman." Chairman Marcus: "Schedule one."
- **18×:** Chairman Marcus: "Does the witness have anything to add?" Witness: "The coin is gone, Chairman. It left this morning." Chairman Marcus: "Let the record show — "
- **25×:** Chairman Marcus, gavel: "The committee will hear one more question, and then the coin will be in contempt."

Past 25× the hearing keeps going: an overtime cycle of sixteen more exchanges comes due every 1.35× of multiplier, in order, forever (the blockchain "with the chain, or the one with the block?" "Yes."; "hypothetically, the boat owns the senator"; page ten of the white paper, blank, read twice; Howe objecting and being sustained back to sleep; "the dip is not available for comment"; "decentralized" in one word: "Elsewhere."; the page's card out of room; both boats in a blind trust that is also a boat).

## Suspense layer

Tension is `1 − 1/x` on the displayed multiplier (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×), so it builds through the 1×–3× window where most rounds end, with a slower log driver keeping long rounds changing. All suspense is keyed to the displayed multiplier and elapsed time, never to the outcome.

- **The stenographer's minutes** stack on the board, one page per exchange: each line said tears a page off her typewriter and it flies up to the top of the stack, which grows toward the ceiling and then packs tighter, with its page count on the plaque. She types faster with tension. From 5× the pages are blank — she has stopped recording the words and is filming vertical with her other hand.
- **The sergeant at arms' watch** is the round's metronome. He checks it more often as tension rises (every seven seconds or so at the start, every two by the end); at high tension he checks it at every exchange.
- **The gallery fills and empties.** Late seats fill as the round starts, and every seat turns over (someone ducks out, someone new sits down) on its own multiplier cycle. Seat cards flip from PUBLIC to BID with the multiplier; from 7× the whole row is one wallet address, a chunk a seat.
- **The witness's hood** casts a deeper shadow as tension rises: the crescent of dark inside the rim widens and the shade spreads across the hood. At no point is a face shown.
- **The page** runs in and out faster, on errands of his own between the ones the lines send him on. The card he carries grows a longer and longer list until it hangs like a receipt.
- **The bail application bouquet** on the clerk's desk: one rolled application per senator, growing with tension, each ribbon tagged with a committee name.
- **The senators reach for phones** they cannot use: the Chairman holds his at arm's length, backwards; Vance takes a call from the family office; Bell's hardware wallet ends up at her ear. Howe's buzzes face down on the bench at every milestone. The HUD's QUORUM reads 4/5 AWAKE until Howe wakes.
- A quiet heartbeat under the dais muzak quickens with tension until the player cashes out.

## Accepted cash-out

The shell confirms the exit; only a confirmed `cashoutX100` triggers this.

Your gallery seat leaves the record: the player (a teal beanie with a YOU tag, front row, right of the aisle) stands and walks out along the row and off the frame; the Chairman bangs the gavel and says "The record will reflect one member of the public had somewhere to be," and the stenographer types it on a gold page that goes up the stack marked YOU. The badge reads **TAKEN AT MY MINUTE · SEE THE RECORD**, the empty seat keeps its PUBLIC card through every flip that follows, and the caption runs TAKEN AT MY MINUTE, SEE THE RECORD.

If the chart keeps going without you, the caption keeps score: **THE RECORD CONTINUES. YOUR MINUTE STANDS.** The hearing continues without the player's seat, the minutes stack, the senators angle ("More room for investors in the public seat", "the public seat is still warm. I checked", "the empty seat has abstained"). A crash after a cash-out reads as dodged: **RUG DODGED — OUTSIDE THE RECORD**, stamped EXCUSED.

## The crash

Keyed to the scene's crash clock (the crash age while the round is crashed), deterministic from the crash point so a replay collapses identically, and front-loaded: live, the next betting window opens about two seconds after a crash, so beats 1–4 land inside two seconds and the final image is complete by six. Whatever was being said is cut off.

1. The witness's phone, on the table beside the hood, buzzes: a flash, a hit-stop with the camera punched in on it, and the screen reads **DEV SOLD**, with the notification held up beside it. He never looks at it. He does not have to.
2. At 0.3 s the gallery's screens all turn the same red in the same frame, and the gallery — one wallet address by now on a long round — goes from PUBLIC to REKT in the same frame; half of it puts its hands on its head.
3. At 0.55 s the stenographer's stack, the game's scoreboard, is pulled down by one page at the bottom, and the whole stack comes off the board as one sheet and folds onto the floor by 1.35 s, loose pages fluttering after it: every word the committee said, gone. The pins stay on the board.
4. At 0.9 s Chairman Marcus, gavel up, says the round's last line: "Let the record show the record is gone." At 1.9 s the gavel comes down and misses the block.
5. At 2.1 s the sergeant at arms' watch beeps: the round ran out of time. He walks to the clerk's desk, picks up the bouquet of bail applications and hands it to the page, who has run in for it (3.4 s). Howe wakes to say "Objection."
6. At 3 s Senator Vance's boats, in the window cutaway at the frame's edge, both turn the same red.
7. The page runs to the witness's table and hands him a card (4.3 s). The card reads: **THE COIN WAS THE RECORD**.
8. At 4.4 s the witness stands — a shadow with no features steps out of the hoodie — and walks out of the frame by 6 s, leaving the hoodie on the chair. Inside the hood: nothing, the hoodie slumped empty on the chair, and the phone still glowing DEV SOLD on the table.

Bell asks "Is my money in the hoodie?" at 5 s; held longer, as in a replay, the room adds "We are adjourned. Somebody find the record." and the captions keep cycling. The stamp reads STRUCK FROM THE RECORD (OFF THE RECORD for a spectator, EXCUSED after an exit) from 2 s. If the next betting window opens before the ending is complete, a crash this scene saw live plays on as an encore over the start of betting — the crashed room, its multiplier, its captions and its cues, on the crash clock advanced by real time — until the final image is complete or 3.5 s have passed (at once if the next round starts running); then a short blink to black covers the room resetting for the next round. A crash after a cash-out is the same collapse seen small from the doorway: the room shrinks into the open double doors of a dim corridor under an EXIT sign, where you stand, back to us, filming it vertical.

## Sound

- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here), `pageAudio({ style: 'elevator', crash: 'slam' })`: dais muzak that opens up with the multiplier; the crash is a slam.
- Cues: a pop for every bubble; the gavel on programme beats (`clang` and `thud`); the stenographer's typing as a rising rattle (`click`) and the carriage bell for every page (`ding`); the page's footsteps (soft `stomp`); a paper rustle (`whoosh`) when the white paper comes out and a snort (`squeak`) when Howe wakes; senator phone buzzes (`notify`) and gallery applause (`crowd`, `cheer` for the boat) on milestones; the sergeant at arms' watch (`tick`); a heartbeat until the exit; on the exit the register, the seat (`creak`) and the door; the boats' window cutaway (nothing). The crash: the slam and the phone's `buzz`, the gallery's `gasp`, the stack falling as one sheet (`whoosh`, then a `thud` as it lands), one gavel miss (`thud`), the watch's double `beep`, the bouquet handed over (`pop`), the card on the table (`click`), the chair (`creak`) and the shadow's footsteps out.

## Lines not to cross

- Every senator, the witness, the page, the stenographer, the sergeant at arms and the gallery are generic cartoons with no likeness to any real person. No real committee, party, government or country is named, shown or implied: the flags are plain blue, the seal is invented, the letters on the dais are unassigned.
- Comedy only: no real legislation, no real securities law advice, nothing in the game is a legal opinion. The boat line is a joke about a bill, not an offer.
- The gallery's loss is the punchline, and it lands on the room's theatrics, not on any person or class of person.
- Credits have no monetary value. Nothing in the game implies the player's own money is at stake, and the label names no opponent.

## How it is built

- `scene.ts`: composition in depth order (the room, the boats, the minutes board, the senators, the dais, their arms and props, the page, the sergeant at arms, the clerk's desk and bouquet, the stenographer, the witness table, the witness and his chair, the gallery, what is in the air, the callouts, the bubbles; the corridor round the doorway after an exit), the clocks (a hit-stop and slow motion on the crash, the crash clock every crash beat reads, and the encore that plays an unfinished ending over the start of betting), the tension mapping, the phase edges and late entry, the bubble placement (kept inside the frame and under the HUD), the exit and the regret lines, the crash choreography, the cues and the HUD.
- `room.ts`: the set. The walls, the seal, the flags, the window and the carpet are painted once into an offscreen layer, the dais front with its name plates and letter board into a cached image; live are the boats, the minutes board (the shingled stack, the pages in flight, the fall as one sheet, the loose pages and the heap), the clerk's desk and bouquet, the witness table, the phone, the DEV SOLD notification, the card and the typewriter.
- `cast.ts`: the five senators facing us (faces with brows, gazes, blinks and talking mouths; torsos as cached sprites; two-bone IK arms holding the gavel, the bill, the white paper, the wallet and the phones; Howe's sleep and wake), the witness from behind (the hoodie, the hood's deepening shadow, the empty hoodie and the shadow that walks out), the stenographer in profile, the sergeant at arms with his watch and his walk for the bouquet, and the page with his distance-driven gait, his errands and his crash run.
- `gallery.ts`: the public row (people from behind, seat turnover, phones filming vertical, applause, the cards from PUBLIC to BID to one wallet address to REKT), the player's seat and walk-out, and the corridor and doorway the dodged crash is seen through.
- `lines.ts`: the words — the opening, the ladder, the overtime cycle, the exit and regret lines, the crash lines, the captions, the badge and the stamps.
- `script.ts`: the dialogue engine (lines keyed to the multiplier, a queue with read holds and beats, a `then` reply, the overtime cycle, settling for late entry). `ink.ts`: the outline, the lettering, the bubbles, the room's geometry and the crash's timeline (`CRASH`, `ENDING_S`). `motion.ts`: springs, easing, deterministic noise and a seeded generator. `kinematics.ts`: two-bone IK and the distance-driven step. `portrait.ts`: the narrow-screen composition.
- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here), in the elevator style with the slam stinger and the cue mapping above. `main.ts`: the shared page shell. `replay.json`: a verified 9.07× round for the gallery.

Every beat follows the displayed multiplier, the round's elapsed time or the crash age; nothing drawn here reads, predicts or changes the committed outcome. The crash's choreography is a pure function of the crash clock (one timeline shared by the set and the cast) and its loose pages are seeded from the crash point, so a replay collapses the same way every time. A scene that opens mid-round settles into place: the ladder said up to the multiplier, the minutes on the board (blank from 5×, your gold page if you left), the senators' props and phones, the gallery's occupants and cards, your seat empty if you cashed out. A crash met late settles straight into its aftermath with no stinger. Long rounds stay bounded: at most five bubbles and seven queued lines, three pages in flight, a stack drawn as one sheet whatever its page count, and the gallery's seats turning over in closed form.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents a close-up of the dais and the hood (the phone during the crash, the card and the empty hoodie after it, the doorway after an exit), a small overview that keeps the room, and the current line with its speaker.
