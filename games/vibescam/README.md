# Vibescam

A token launch livestream in three panes. On the left, **the founder's webcam**: a dark den under an LED strip, a pink **$VIBE** neon, a whiteboard with the plan (a hockey stick, "tokenomics: later", "EXIT: Q?"), a shelf with a participation trophy, sticky notes (SHIP IT, NO REFUNDS, Q2 = ???, NOT A RUG), a door marked FOUNDER MODE · DO NOT DISTURB, and a pale desk with a keyboard, an energy drink, a big red **SHIP IT** button and, on the floor beside it, a duffel bag tagged **LP**. In the chair, in a backwards cap, a headset with a mic boom and a $VIBE hoodie: **the founder**, a professional who has not slept, has not coded, and has vibes. In the middle, **the screen share**: VibeCode (a prompt box and the assistant's generated code), the landing page of **VibeOS** (a product that does not exist) and the $VIBE chart. On the right, **the stream chat**, with your wallet pinned at the bottom.

The multiplier is the launch. Every step up the curve he types another prompt, the assistant ships another feature, a badge lands on the landing page, he smacks SHIP IT, and the chat, the holders, the viewers, the warnings and his sweat climb with the number. The token has no tokenomics, no sell button, a padlock drawn open, a roadmap to Mars and a tokenomics pie with one slice. Under the sticker on the button, it says RUG.

## Direction

The comedy is a founder pitching, with total confidence, a product that an AI assistant is making up in real time, to a chat that can see all of it. Every claim is absurd and every one of them ships; the assistant is cheerfully literal ("Certainly!") and does exactly what it is asked, including removing the sell button and hiding the warning that the liquidity is null. The tells are all on screen the whole round: the pie, the padlock, the peeling sticker, the packed bag. Nobody in the chat is fooled, and the number goes up anyway.

- The founder never lies about anything. He asks the assistant for it and reads the result out loud as an achievement.
- The assistant is the only honest party in the stream, and it is honest in the most unhelpful way possible.
- The chat is right about everything, early, and holding.
- Nothing in the game implies a real product, a real token, or a return: credits have no monetary value and $VIBE is a cartoon.

## Suspense layer

Tension is `1 − 1/x` on the displayed multiplier (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×), so it builds through the 1×–3× window where most rounds end, with a slower log driver for the things that keep growing in long rounds. All of it is keyed to the displayed multiplier and the round clock, never to the outcome.

- **The founder** types faster, slumps closer to the camera, grins wider, sweats from about 2× and twitches from 3×; his blinks stretch out and stop past 0.75 tension (the chat notices). At high tension he glances at the button every few seconds.
- **The sticker** on SHIP IT curls at one corner with the tension, showing a sliver of the red under it. A warning light on the plinth blinks faster.
- **The LED strip** along the wall (and under the desk) turns from cyan through magenta to red; the ring light flickers at high tension; the neon flickers.
- **The LP bag** on the floor bulges with the number (a log driver), and bills poke out of the zipper past about 10×.
- **The IDE** keeps generating lines on its own between features (`// TODO: tokenomics`, `delete window.audit;`, `user.canSell = false; // UX`), faster as the number climbs, with squiggles under the warnings and a warnings count that only goes up.
- **The landing page** stacks badges, strikes the SELL button, resolves the tokenomics spinner to one slice, draws the padlock open and swinging, and fills the phone with vapor.
- **The chart strip** shows the $VIBE price, market cap, holders (`900 · x^1.35`) and the LP status; the viewer count in the top bar climbs with the number and the chat posts faster.
- **The readouts** at the foot: SHIPPED 07 · 2,429 WARNINGS, HOLDERS 7,148 · LP LOCKED (HEART).

## Accepted cash-out

The shell confirms the sale; only a confirmed `cashoutX100` triggers this.

Your wallet sells: the pinned row at the foot of the chat flips to **you · SOLD @ 2.31× · +115 credits · out before the rug**, you post "sold. gm." in the chat, and the badge over the cam reads **SOLD · PAPER HANDS, SER** with the exit multiplier and the credits. The founder reacts: "Who sold? Chat, who sold? …Unfollowed. Blocked. Anyway: prompt."

If the number keeps going without you, the caption keeps score (**YOU SOLD. HE DIDN'T NOTICE.**, then **THE VIBE CONTINUES WITHOUT YOU**) and the regret lines come at 1.5×, 2×, 3×, 5× and 10× past your exit: "Paper hands, ser. The product doesn't even exist yet.", "A wallet has exited. I have marked it 'not a believer'.", "Imagine selling before the pivot.", "The seller is up anyway. I have decided not to tell him.", "We don't talk about the seller." A crash after a sale reads as dodged: **RUG DODGED — SOLD THE VIBE FIRST**, your avatar in the pinned row puts on shades, and the stamp reads **SOLD THE VIBE FIRST**.

## The crash

Keyed to the scene's crash clock (the view's `crashAge`, carried on through the encore below), deterministic from the crash point so a replay rugs identically. The beats are front-loaded for the live hold, which moves on to the next betting phase about two seconds after the crash: the smack and the vapor inside a second, every beat by 4.7 s, the final image complete at 5.6 s.

1. **0 s:** the chart drops to the floor in one frame (a red flash, a hit-stop, the camera punches in on the button). He stares at the camera. The chat starts flooding: RUGGED, LIQUIDITY GONE, SITE IS 404, HE DELETED EVERYTHING, it was vapor, the sticker said RUG, I KNEW IT (didn't sell).
2. **0.22 s:** "Prompt: remove liquidity." He peels the sticker off the button. Under it: **RUG**.
3. **0.6 s:** he smacks it. The button sinks, the desk shivers, the can falls over.
4. **0.85 s:** the landing page dissolves into vapor, seventy soft puffs rising off it, and 404s: *This site can't be reached. (It never could.) ERR_VAPORWARE*.
5. **1.25 s:** "Certainly! Liquidity removed. Repository deleted. Is there anything else?" The code falls out of the IDE line by line; the terminal types `rm -rf ./product && git push --force`.
6. **1.6 s:** HOLDERS 1 (dev). LP $0.00 (lol). The viewers drain.
7. **2 s:** the shades go on. "It was a vibe. Vibes are non-refundable."
8. **2.4 s:** he rolls the chair left, stoops and grabs the LP bag.
9. **2.65 s:** the lights die: the ring light, the neon tube by tube (the I lingers), the LED strip to red. The sticky notes let go one by one and plane down to the floor.
10. **2.8 s:** he stands; **3.1 s:** he walks out through the door with the bag, the door opening as he reaches it, the chair swivelling empty behind him.
11. **3.7 s:** STREAM ENDED · The creator has deleted their account. "Stream ended. The creator has deleted their account. Have a great day!" Chat is disabled. The outcome stamp: **EXIT LIQUIDITY** (you held), **SOLD THE VIBE FIRST** (you sold), **JUST VIBING** (you watched).

If the next betting phase arrives while the ending is still playing, and this scene saw the crash live, the ending plays on as an encore on the scene's own clock (at most 3.5 s, cut short if the next round starts running): the crash caption, the stamp, the crash multiplier and the crashed music hold, the lines and cues continue, then a short blink to black covers the den resetting for the next round. A crash met late, or on a first frame, settles into its final image with no cues and the quiet crash.

## Sound

- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here) in its `synthwave` style — glossy and confident, tightening with the multiplier — with the `static` crash stinger (the stream cutting out) and tape stop.
- Cues: his keystrokes (`click`, quickening with the tension), the IDE's own lines (`tick`), each line said (`pop`; the assistant `beep`), the warning (`buzz`), the smack of SHIP IT on every feature (`thud` and `kaching`), chat posts (a quiet `tick`), DMs (`notify`), milestone stingers on the rungs, the sale (`cashout`); and the crash: the sticker's `ratchet`, the smack's `punch` and `thud`, the vapor's `hiss` and `whoosh`, the deletion's `zap`, the holders' `buzz`, the shades' `click`, the bag's `yeet`, the lights' `zap`, the chair's `creak`, footsteps (`thud`), the door (`door`) and the stream ending (`beep`).

## Lines not to cross

- The founder, the assistant and every name in the chat are generic cartoons with no likeness to any real person, product, company or assistant. VibeOS and $VIBE are fictional; [REDACTED] is redacted from the game too.
- The game is satire of a scam, not an instruction for one: nothing it shows works, which is the joke, and nothing in it implies a real token, a product, a listing or a return.
- Credits have no monetary value. Nothing in the game implies the player's own money is at stake, and the label names no opponent.

## How it is built

- `scene.ts`: the clocks (real `dt` clamped to 0.1 s, a 0.15 s hit-stop, slow motion and a camera punch on the button at the crash; the scene-owned crash clock and the encore into betting), tension and the log driver, the confirmed sale, phase edges with late entry and quiet crashes, the dialogue's beats (`ask:` types the prompt, `ship:` lands the feature, the badge and the smack; features a fast curve dropped from the queue still ship quietly), the bubble layout (each bubble placed once, beside its speaker, by a search that keeps it inside the frame and off the HUD, the chat, his face, the DMs and each other), the cues, the draw order (the den, the founder's body, the desk, his arms over it, the lighting; the screen share; the chat; the chrome; the bubbles) and the HUD (multiplier, caption, badge, readouts, stamp) and the portrait compositor.
- `den.ts`: the webcam's set and the three panes' layout. The static wall and floor (panels, the whiteboard with the plan, the shelf, the door frame, the rug) are painted once into sprites; live on top: the LED strip and its wash (hue from the tension), the neon with per-tube flicker and death, the door (opening on a spring as he nears it), the sticky notes and their seeded falls, the ring light, the chair (rolled, pushed back, swivelling), the desk with the keyboard, the can (hopping on a smack, tipping at the crash), the SHIP IT button on its plinth with the warning light, the sticker (curl and peel) and RUG under it, the LP bag with its bulge and bills, the dark and the screen glow. `BEATS` for the crash live here.
- `founder.ts`: the rig. Seated facing the camera: a lean and a slump on springs, a nervous bob and a typing tap as integrated phases, pupils on a look target (the screen, the camera for a line, the button, the chat for a DM, the bag), a grin and brows on springs, sweat, a twitch, blinks that stop, two-bone IK arms from the shoulders to the keys, to a talking gesture, to the button for the smack (a wind-up, the contact as a one-frame event, a hold and the return). At the crash every pose is a closed form of the crash clock: the stare, the peel, the smack, the shades, the chair rolled left and the stoop to the bag, standing, and a distance-driven gait (`walkingFoot`) out through the door with the bag swinging in hand, so a late entry settles into the same frame a live viewer saw.
- `screens.ts`: the screen share. The IDE (the prompt typed letter by letter, "thinking…", generated code sliding in with squiggles under the warnings, a filler pool between features, the status bar, and at the crash the lines dropping out of the pane and the `rm -rf`), the landing page (hero, tagline, testimonials ticker, badges that flow in rows, BUY, the SELL button's strike-through and removal, the tokenomics spinner and the one-slice pie, the padlock drawn open and swinging, the roadmap, the phone that never loads, the vapor ribbon, the seeded vapor dissolve and the 404) and the chart strip (a sample of the multiplier every half second of round time, thinned as it fills; price, market cap, holders, LP; the cliff at the crash).
- `chat.ts`: the stream chrome and the chat: the top bar (LIVE/ENDED, the viewers), the pane frames, the chat column (pools by tier, a seeded generator, no line twice running, posts that quicken with the tension, the flood), the DMs that slide onto the cam, your pinned row (holding, watching, SOLD, EXIT LIQUIDITY, shades through a dodged crash) and the STREAM ENDED card.
- `pitch.ts`: the words and the features (prompt, code, badge, page change), the chat pools, the DMs, the captions, the regrets, the crash lines and the stamps.
- `script.ts`: the dialogue engine (a queue keyed to the multiplier, a 2.6 s hold per speaker, an overtime cycle, unscheduled lines, late-entry settling). `ink.ts`: the outline, lettering, bubbles, shapes and resolution-aware sprites. `motion.ts`: springs, easing, noise and the seeded generator. `kinematics.ts`: two-bone IK and the walking foot. `portrait.ts`: the narrow-screen composition.
- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (`synthwave`, crash `static`; never edited here). `main.ts`: the shared page shell. `replay.json`: a verified 9.07× round for the gallery, with this game's ID.
- `founder.test.mjs`: a headless test beside the game (bone lengths through typing, talking and the smack; one contact per smack; the walk-out's closed form; planted soles at three frame rates; a settled late entry matching a stepped crash; blinks that stop). It is not part of the source pack; run it with `node --test games/vibescam/founder.test.mjs`.

The founder, the screens, the chat, the lights and the HUD all follow the displayed multiplier and the round clock; the crash follows the scene's crash clock and is seeded from the crash point (`mulberry32(x100 · 7 + 3)` for the den, `· 11 + 7` for the vapor and the code's fall), and every crash pose is a closed-form function of the crash age, so a replay or a late entry draws the same frame. Nothing drawn here reads or changes the committed outcome. A scene opened mid-round settles straight into place: every feature below the multiplier shipped, the badges on the page, the chart filled, nine lines in the chat, the DMs read, the last line or two up; after a sale your row already reads SOLD; after a crash the aftermath is shown as it stands with no cues. Long rounds stay bounded: at most 40 code lines, 24 chat messages, 3 DMs and 5 bubbles, a chart that thins as it fills, and overtime lines every 1.35× forever.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents a close-up of the founder's cam (the cam and the screens at the crash, the chat's pinned row after a sale), a small overview of the whole stream, and the current line.

Run `npm run preview -- vibescam` from the repository root and open the printed URL. Join with 50 local credits, then cash out during a running round to sell before the rug.

All visuals are original Canvas 2D shapes and text; sound uses the repository's shared procedural helper (`clips.json` is `{}`). The founder, VibeOS, $VIBE and everyone in the chat are fictional. Credits have no monetary value. No third-party assets are included. The game is an open remix origin (`gallery.json`).
