# Thin Ice

A skater crosses a frozen lake under the aurora on 100× leverage: the ice is thin liquidity, and the lake is full of frozen bagholders who went through before her. A `YOU` tag rides over her while you have a bet on. She speeds up with the displayed multiplier, her strokes follow the distance she covers, her reflection glides beneath her, and the ice around her fails faster and faster: water shows through, cracks spread from her skates with a creak, and the `LIQUIDATION ENGINE`, a drone with a loudhailer, lasers her `LIQ PRICE` onto the ice a stride ahead of her, closer as the tension rises. Between the caption rungs come near misses: at 1.15×, 1.5×, 2×, 3× and on up the ladder, a crack races in at her from behind and runs out of steam just short of her back blade, and she flinches. The bagholders thaw as she passes and get a hand up through a melting hole, feeling for an ankle, higher the more leverage she is on, while the drone heckles her with lines that get meaner up the ladder (spectators get called `ANON`) and closes in on her. An accepted cashout closes the long (`CLOSED THE LONG. NOT A JEET`, then `NOT A JEET (IS A JEET)` as the round runs on without her) and carves her toward the far shore, where she climbs onto the snow by an `EXIT` / `LIQUIDITY` signpost to a whistle and confetti while the drone sulks back and gets sourer the higher the round goes (`PAPER HANDS. SAD!`). The lake does not empty: a translucent `NEXT LONG` skates on along her line, lap after lap, and if it is in the picture when the round ends, the ice takes it instead: `DODGED!`. The crash breaks the ice along seeded rays and rings into floes: the inner wedges tip their points under and slide away to open a real hole, the outer rings drift on open water, three of the bagholders surface on the floes with their signs (`BUY THE DIP`, `STILL EARLY`, `DCA BABY`…), and the drone reads the verdict over the hole. Between rounds the lake refreezes: the break and the cracks fade back into the ice, and she climbs out of the water or glides down off the bank to her mark.

- `motion.ts`: the same toolkit as the other games: exact damped springs, easing, deterministic noise, a seeded generator.
- `audio.ts`: the page's sound (the shell's copy, the same in every game). The scene asks for the `synthwave` style with a `shatter` crash: an arpeggiated night drive that opens up with the tension, a hiss on each push off the back skate, a creak with each burst of cracks (every 2 s at the start, every second by 3×, while you are still in), a creak and a heartbeat for each near miss, a squawk (`beep`) each time the drone starts a new line, a rising ding and airhorn at the caption's milestones, the cash-out's register and fanfare with a whistle as she climbs out, and at the crash a creak as the ice snaps, the shatter, the splash as she goes in, bubbles while she swims, a gasp as the bagholders surface and the drone's siren over the hole.
- `ice.ts`: the lake. The foreshortened plane with frozen-in streaks and a sweeping sheen, the bagholders frozen under it (seeded by index at fixed world positions, each with its own pose and tilt), skate trails, a crack network of seeded random walks with branches that grow in, the near-miss racers (a crack that runs from one point to another in a set time, easing out, its tip glinting), the thinning under her, and the shatter: floes cut from rays and rings that separate (55, 25 and 8 px by ring), the inner wedges tipping on their outer edge so their points go under, and bob over rippling water. The thaw: each bagholder she passes wakes at a rate set by how close she skates and the tension, darkens and opens its eyes, and the ice melts open beside it before a blue hand comes up through the hole, reaching higher with the leverage; when the ice breaks they let go and freeze back over. The surfacers: at the shatter, seeded by the crash point, one bagholder per side of the middle ring and one on the outer ring come up out of their floes on overshooting springs, soaked and shivering, each holding a sign, and ride the floes' bob and tilt. The refreeze fades the break, cracks and trails out before the next round and sinks the surfacers back under.
- `engine.ts`: the `LIQUIDATION ENGINE` drone: rotors, a red light whose blink phase is integrated so it runs faster the closer it gets without ever jumping (steady under reduced motion), a loudhailer with sound rings while it talks, a name plate, and a speech bubble under it, kept over the far shore and clear of the HUD. It hovers by the far shore between rounds, flies in over her with the tension (the anticipation the crash pays off), leans into its own motion, backs off when her exit is accepted and comes down over the hole at the crash. Its lines follow the caption ladder, call a spectator `ANON`, and after her exit turn to sour grapes keyed to how far the round has run past it.
- `skater.ts`: the stride rig. Each stroke pushes off the back skate and glides on the front one through two-bone legs that stay on the ice, the stroke advancing with the distance covered (so it never strokes in place, and settles into a two-blade stance when she stops), arms swing opposite, the torso leans into the speed, a scarf trails on a chain of eased links; the carve to shore, the shades, the brace (a hard brake, a crouch and arms thrown out when the ice snaps), the plunge that carries what speed is left into the water, the buoyant dunk and bob-back, the shivering swim, and the way back to her mark for the next round.
- `scene.ts`: the sky with stars, aurora and moon, parallax mountains and the pine-lined shore with the exit sign that springs up where she climbs out (and back down for the next round), the camera that follows her, spray, breath, splash and confetti particles, snow, the `YOU` and `NEXT LONG` tags, the `LIQ PRICE` line, the ghost skater (drawn off-screen and laid over the lake translucent and frost-tinted), the HUD (captions, multiplier, this round's distance, the secured badge), the sound cues and the round-phase logic. The crash is choreographed: the ice snaps in a ring round whoever is out on it, centred where her brake will stop her, with a creak, and the liquidation price catches her; it gives 0.14 s later (the fuse), the picture holds for a 0.15 s hit-stop with the camera punched 10% in on the hole for another 0.3 s, the plunge runs at a third speed with the splash landing when she does, and the camera eases back out. A crash during her carve to shore breaks in her wake, and once she is safe the camera pans only as far as it must to keep the break in the picture. A crash met late settles straight into the aftermath: the hole, the floes, the survivors already up, no effects.
- `main.ts`: SDK connection, round lifecycle, the player's bet and cashout, input controls and the animation loop.
- `replay.json`: a verifiable settled example round (5.07×) used by the gallery.

Tension is `1 − 1/x` (0.33 at 1.5×, 0.5 at 2×, 0.67 at 3×, 0.9 at 10×), so the escalation lives in the 1×–3× window where most rounds end; her speed and the crack bursts also follow the log of the multiplier, so long rounds keep changing. The crack cadence, the thinning, the near misses, the `LIQ PRICE` gap, her face, the bagholders' reach, the drone's approach and the music all follow the displayed multiplier; nothing in the presentation reads or changes the committed outcome. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off the screen shake, the hit-stop, the slow motion (including the near misses' beat), the camera punch-in, the drone's blinking light and the twinkle. Sound is opt-in through the Sound button and stays off until it is pressed.

Run `npm run dev` from the repository root and open the Thin Ice URL it prints. Join with 50 local credits, then cash out during a running round to close the long and head for the shore. `npm run build` produces the three publishable files in `dist/thin-ice/`.
