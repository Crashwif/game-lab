# Blanket Champ

A stamina crash game starring a couple tucked under a quilt. Two expressive heads peek out on pillows: a red-sweatband champ who goes from smug to sweaty to exhausted, and a partner supplying the side-eye. Four distinct feet kick at the other end: striped red socks and bare feet with heels, arches and toes. The quilt has a turned-down edge, stitched folds and two broad moving rises, so the characters read as a couple in bed. A visible arm connects the champ to his grip on the headboard. Behind the bed, three rows of Wojak fans with foam fingers and signs, a commentary booth with a LIVE light, a bookie under a live odds board, and a wall TV tuned to DEGEN NEWS. The quilt's tempo climbs with the displayed multiplier and the room reacts, with a new beat every two or three seconds through the opening: the champ's brow comes down from 1.3×, the headboard knocks a new crack into the wall at 1.67×, 2×, 2.5× and onwards with a puff of plaster, the cat gets up and trots off at 2× (the partner rolls her eyes), she gets her phone out to check her charts at 2.2×, the glass of water skids off the nightstand around 2.3× (another eye-roll), the champ starts to tremble from 2.5× and panics around 3× while she yawns, then the crowd does the wave, the neighbour's fist comes through the wall at 4×, the bed legs buckle at 6× and past 10× the fire brigade is at the window. The bookie's board prices FINISH BEFORE 2×, 5×, 10× and 20×, the odds shortening with the number and each line flipping to VOID as he passes it; the TV runs a BREAKING headline for every stage (HE HAS BEGUN, NEIGHBOURS FILE COMPLAINT, BED FRAME RATED JUNK, FIRE BRIGADE EN ROUTE) and a bulletin when the glass goes or the fist comes through. Only a player with a stake sees their supporter in gold under a YOU tag; a spectator sees an ordinary fan. An accepted cashout stands your supporter up and walks him to the bookie for the bag, the shades and a burst of confetti, while the round plays on and the caption keeps you at peace with it as he goes past your exit (DEAL WITH IT, PROFIT IS PROFIT, JEETED. NO REGRETS); a finish after you sold reads DODGED. The crash is the champ finishing: the picture holds for a hit-stop with the camera punched in on the quilt, then runs slow while the quilt settles, the heads slump, the feet relax, a puff, an arm flops over the side and gives a thumbs-up, small falling confetti for a legendary number, the fans still holding tickets put their heads in their hands, the remaining lines on the board pay out, the TV breaks the news, and the cat walks back in holding a tiny placard (NGMI, or PAPER if you sold first).

- `motion.ts`: the shared toolkit: exact damped springs, easing, deterministic noise, a seeded generator.
- `room.ts`: the presentation's tension, the bedroom, quilt with moving seams and a hanging hem, character layering, connected headboard grip, lamp, the glass skidding on a spring, the cat walking on its legs and its placard, fist, cracks, buckling legs, the wall TV and its sliding headlines, the finish, puffs and confetti.
- `sleepers.ts`: pillows, expressive faces (the champ's determination and panic, the partner's side-eye, eye-rolls, yawn and phone), sweatband, sweat drops, the champ's tremble near the top, staggered socked and bare-foot kicks, and the partner's speech bubbles ("socks stay ON.", "brb, checking charts", "even the cat's leaving", "bro is buffering", "was that it?").
- `crowd.ts`: the bleachers, the fans (bob, wave, hype, head in hands), your supporter and his YOU tag, his walk to the bookie and back, the bookie, the bag and the confetti, the live odds board, the commentary booth.
- `scene.ts`: composition, the fire brigade, the headline ladder, the finish choreography (hit-stop, slow motion, a camera punch toward the quilt), the HUD (captions, commentary line, multiplier, stamina clock, the secured badge) and the round-phase logic.
- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of `scripts/shell/audio.ts`): a phonk set that climbs with the tempo, milestone dings and airhorns on the caption ladder, a thud on every headboard beat, a shatter for the glass, a punch for the fist, a siren for the brigade, a cheer for the wave, a notification for each headline, a squeak for the cat's return, a coin at the bookie, the cash-out fanfare, and a sad trombone (or a roar for a finish you were not holding through) with the tape stop.
- `main.ts`: SDK connection, round lifecycle, the player's bet and cashout, input controls and the animation loop.
- `replay.json`: a verifiable settled example round (10.31×) used by the gallery.

Everything below the faces and above the feet stays under the duvet; the humour is innuendo, expressions and the room. The result stamp sits on the quilt so it leaves both faces visible. Tempo, crowd noise, the props, the odds, the headlines and the commentary all follow the displayed multiplier; the tension (and the music it drives) is 1 − 1/x, so 1× to 3× sweeps from calm to two thirds. Nothing in the presentation reads the committed outcome before the finish, or changes it. A round met late settles into place quietly: the board at the number, the cat already back, no replayed finish. Between rounds nothing jumps: the quilt fluffs back up, the arm slides back under, the old cracks and broken glass fade out and your supporter walks back to his seat. The published game uses full animation regardless of browser motion preferences. Sound is opt-in through the Sound button.

Run `npm run dev` from the repository root and open the Blanket Champ URL it prints. Join with 50 local credits, then cash out during a running round to collect. `npm run build` produces the three publishable files in `dist/blanket-champ/`.

## Visual direction and small screens

The scene now introduces physical presentation acts at 32, 52, 75, 100, 125 and 145 seconds, followed by bounded recurring acts for unusually long rounds. New props accompany changes in character effort, with the quilt's tempo easing by up to 12% mid-act before renewed activity; the tension never eases with it. The props fade in, crossfade from one act to the next and fade out at the finish or a cash-out. `acts.ts` reads elapsed time only; it cannot choose an outcome or promise that a round will last this long.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents an enlarged character/detail view, a small overview that preserves the location, and a readable current line from the actual dialogue/chat/monitor state. The canonical shell still owns controls, round state and accepted cashouts.
