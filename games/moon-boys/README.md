# Moon Boys

The second 3D reference, in raw WebGL2 with no libraries: a memecoin rocket to the moon, launched from a trailer park. $MOONBOYS sold out, so ninety-six holders ride the outside of the upper stage, clinging to the hull in meme-faced helmets; your astronaut clings at the front in a lime visor. Mission control is a voice chat in the corner where the dev says gm, the mod is asleep, mom says dinner is ready and the holders join and leave the call as they climb aboard and let go. The rocket climbs with the multiplier from mom's backyard (the pad, a scaffold tower, the trailer with a dish pointed at the moon, the dog house, the kiddie pool, a car on bricks, mom at the door with a plate) into a sky that darkens into stars, and everything about the trip is slightly wrong. At 1.3× the strap-on boosters separate with the snipers riding them down (SNIPERS DUMPED). At 1.8× the camera looks down and the earth is a plate with an ice wall round the rim (FLAT EARTH CONFIRMED). At 2× the core stage goes (BUNDLED: 60% of the supply). At 2.4× the moon, a painted disc that always faces the camera and grows as the multiplier climbs, opens its eyes and follows the rocket with them. At 3.3× a boom mic dips into frame. At 4.2× two wires glint above the nose. At 4.6× a bird crosses with a lens for an eye and a red light on its back (BIRDS AREN'T REAL: SEC DRONE). At 5.5× a giant stagehand's glove reaches in from the edge of the world and nudges the moon, which wobbles. At 6.9× the moon winks (NICE). Past 10× it sweats. Holders let go in jeet waves at every milestone and tumble away behind, a few of them named (INFLUENCER SOLD, MY COUSIN SOLD, WHALE SOLD), and a SUS meter on the mission panel fills with every oddity noticed while the HOPIUM gauge drains into COPIUM and then PURE DELUSION. An accepted cash-out is a bail-out: your astronaut jumps, the parachute opens and you drift down behind the climbing rocket under a BAILED badge with your payout. The crash has beats. In flight: the right wire snaps with a twang, a short freeze and a punch of the lens, the engines die, everyone lets go screaming and the rocket tumbles while the studio lights come up into a white-out. Then the cut: a soundstage (STAGE 69) with a model rocket on a crash mat under its wires, the moon a plywood prop on a stand with a shocked face, the earth a painted floor cloth, three studio lights, a craft services table, the SEC's bird perched on a lamp, the crew on the mat, a heap of holders, and the dev in the director's chair: a lizard in a suit. The clapperboard snaps shut (CUT!). The moon prop tips over and shows its back (YOU GOT MOONED). The dev hops onto a golf cart and leaves with the bag, dragging the earth away by its corner with three holders still on it (RUG PULLED). The crew climb onto the fallen moon and raise a WAGMI flag (COMMUNITY TAKEOVER: THE MOON). If you bailed, you stand in the foreground with your parachute bundled up and your bag, under CALLED IT. Captions: T-MINUS WHENEVER, LIFTOFF (TRUST ME), NUMBER GO UP, HODL THE RAILING, DIAMOND GLOVES, DON'T LOOK DOWN, THE WIRE IS A FEATURE, THE MOON IS BIDDING, NICE, NEXT STOP URANUS, THE MOON IS SWEATING, STILL FAKE STILL PUMPING, THIS IS FINE, WE ARE THE MOON NOW, with SNIPERS DUMPED, FLAT EARTH CONFIRMED, BUNDLED, THE MOON BLINKED, IS THAT A BOOM MIC, WHY IS THERE A WIRE, BIRDS AREN'T REAL, HAND OF DEV and JEETS LET GO as they happen. Crash captions: RUG PULLED and NGMI, THE MOON WAS FAKE and IT WAS A SOUNDSTAGE, CALLED IT, FAILED TO LAUNCH for a 1.00× round, then CUT!, YOU GOT MOONED, DEV LEFT WITH THE BAG (AND THE EARTH) and COMMUNITY TAKEOVER: THE MOON.

- `motion.ts`: the shared toolkit: exact damped springs, easing, deterministic noise, a seeded generator.
- `audio.ts`: the page's sound, the shell's copy (see the repository README): opt-in through the Sound button, synthesised, nothing loaded. The scene runs it in `synthwave` with a `trombone` crash, and cues it from what it already tracks: a `whoosh` and a `boom` at liftoff, a `tick` on each beat of the climb, the milestone dings up the ladder, `kaching` as the boosters separate, `boo` and the odd `scream` for a jeet wave, `gasp` for the flat earth, `pop` as the moon's eyes open, `creak` for the boom mic and the wires, `camera` for the bird, `squeak` as the glove nudges the moon, `ding` for the wink, `notify` for a line in the chat, `zap` and the cash-out fanfare as you bail, then `scream` at the snap, `laugh` at the cut, `punch` for the clapperboard, `thud` as the moon tips, `whoosh` and `engine` as the cart leaves and `cheer` for the flag.
- `sound.ts`: the game's own sounds on the engine's bus: a rumble bed that follows the thrust and the twang of the wire snapping.
- `math3d.ts`: vectors as tuples, column-major matrices, look-at and perspective, and projection to HUD pixels.
- `meshes.ts`: every model built at start-up from lathes, ellipsoids, boxes, discs and quads: the boosters, the core with its swept fins and five engines, the upper stage, the capsule, the nose and its antenna, the portholes, the astronauts in three poses with a visor that carries the instance's face, the parachute, the flat earth and the moon as plates with a printed top and (for the moon) a printed back, the boom mic, the glove, the bird and its wings, the lizard, the golf cart, the clapperboard and its stick, the studio light, the director's chair, the flag, the dish, the trailer, the dog house, the pool, the tower, the pad and the bag.
- `atlas.ts`: one Canvas 2D texture: eight faces, the moon and the flat earth painted as props, the back of the moon, two mouths and the prints (the coin sticker, HOPIUM, COPIUM, WAGMI, the clapperboard, STAGE 69, SEC, DEV, EXIT, DOGE).
- `gl.ts`, `shaders.ts`, `render.ts`: WebGL2 plumbing and three programs. The sky is a gradient that darkens into a star field with altitude, with the sun, or a flat studio wall. The lit instanced program draws everything solid, with faces and prints from the atlas, painted props and unlit lamps as materials, and a flag that waves in the vertex shader. The sprite program draws the flame as streaks, the smoke, the glows, the sweat and the sparks.
- `rocket.ts`: the rocket in its own frame, the stage separations, the holders' grid on the hull and their waves, the named holders, your bail-out on the parachute, the exhaust and the launch cloud, and the in-flight tumble at the crash.
- `world.ts`: the backyard spaceport, the flat earth, the moon's placement and its face, the boom mic and the bird in camera space, the glove, the wires, and the sky and lighting by altitude.
- `crash.ts`: the crash beats and the soundstage, seeded from the crash point so a replay falls the same way. The reveal keeps playing for a few seconds into the next round's betting when that opens sooner.
- `feed.ts`: the mission control voice chat, seeded so a replay prints the same lines, and the lines that come out at each rung of the multiplier (a scene joining late skips the ones already said).
- `hud.ts`: meme captions, the mission control panel (altitude, the HOPIUM gauge, the holders aboard, the SUS meter), labels pinned to things in the world, the vignette.
- `fallback.ts`: a Canvas 2D side view of the same launch for browsers without WebGL2, so a round is never blank. It also covers a lost WebGL context (a GPU reset, a phone backgrounding the tab) until the browser restores it and the renderer uploads everything again.
- `scene.ts`: the camera (an orbit round the pad while the round loads, a chase cam that dips for the flat earth once it flies), composition, the HUD and the round-phase logic.
- `main.ts`: SDK connection, round lifecycle, the player's bet and cashout, input controls and the animation loop.
- `replay.json`: a verifiable settled example round (7.77×) used by the gallery.

The page keeps a single Canvas 2D element. WebGL2 renders into an offscreen canvas at up to 1.5× device pixels, and each frame is copied in underneath the HUD, so the integration, the poster capture and the Studio upload are the same as for the 2D games. Everything in the picture follows the displayed multiplier, and nothing in the presentation changes the committed outcome. Everyone in it is a cartoon: astronauts in helmets, a lizard in a suit, a bird with a lens. The conspiracies are the joke, and the joke is on the dev. `prefers-reduced-motion` removes the camera shake and roll, the flashes, the snap's freeze, slow motion and punch, the sweat, the sparks and the flag's wave, and calms the exhaust.

Run `npm run dev` from the repository root and open the Moon Boys URL it prints. Join with 50 local credits, then cash out during a running round to bail out. `npm run build` produces the three publishable files in `dist/moon-boys/`.
