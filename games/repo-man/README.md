# Repo Man

A suburban cul-de-sac at 4:07 am. A two-story house, one lit window upstairs. In the driveway, a leased lime-green Lamborghini with a paper dealer plate. At the curb, a flatbed tow truck idling, amber beacons off (for now), and **Gary**: clipboard, coffee thermos, reflector vest over a shirt that says **GARY'S TOWING & LIQUIDATION**, sleeve tattoo of a barcode. Upstairs, the **degen** at the window, blinds open one slat, laptop open on the bed behind him, chart green.

The multiplier is how much Gary reclaims. Each milestone he unbolteds more of the house. He has a checklist. The checklist is the loan agreement.

## Direction

The comedy is Gary: a man doing a terrible job with complete dignity and a form for everything. The horror is the degen's, and it is self-inflicted — the chart behind him is green the entire round, and that is the joke. Nothing is funny or sad like a man watching his own house get itemized by someone holding a clipboard and being entirely correct.

- Gary speaks from the clipboard. He is respectful. He is thorough. He is the most reasonable person in the game.
- The degen has no lines until the cash-out, and then only one.
- The house reacts with physics: the TV leaves a patch, the porch was modular, the sprinklers run on a schedule nobody owns anymore.
- Gary never gloats. He nods. Respect between professionals.

## Dialogue ladder

Keyed to the displayed multiplier. Gary's lines are from the clipboard, a checklist item per beat, queued so a bubble stays up at least 2.6 seconds before the same speaker's next line replaces it.

- **Betting:** Gary thumbs through the contract by dome light: "Collateral: vehicle. Secondary: fixtures. Tertiary: the vibe." He writes "the vibe" down.
- **1.2×:** The hook settles onto the Lambo's frame with a click the degen hears through the glass.
- **1.5×:** Gary checks the contract again. The clause is highlighted. There is a clause for the garage door opener.
- **1.9×:** The Lambo rises an inch. The alarm chirps twice and gives up — the battery is the bank's too.
- **2.3×:** Gary unhooks the garden hose. "Secondary: fixtures." He coils it.
- **2.8×:** The TV through the living-room window goes dark mid-paragraph and comes off the wall with a suction pull. The wall patch is shaped like a mount.
- **3.4×:** The kid's bike. Gary pauses. He writes something on the clipboard. He takes the bike anyway. He adds a bow to the handlebars — from the gift wrap roll in his truck — because Gary is not a monster.
- **4.1×:** The smart fridge's screen — the one showing a family photo — displays a firmware notice: **OWNERSHIP TRANSFERRED**. It plays a jingle. The jingle is cheerful. This is the worst sound in the game.
- **5×:** The degen is now at the window with both blinds open, laptop still on the bed behind him, chart still green. Gary looks up at him. Gary nods.
- **7×:** The sprinkler system runs its morning cycle on a schedule the degen no longer owns. The lawn waters itself for the bank.
- **10×:** Gary unhooks the house numbers: 4, 0, 7. He leaves a Post-it: "you were 407 days late."
- **14×:** The for-sale sign grows from the lawn like a sapling in a timelapse, the realtor's face already smiling.
- **22×:** Gary begins on the porch. The porch comes off in one piece. It was a modular porch. Who knew.
- **36×:** The house's foundation is exposed: the concrete contractor stamped it years ago with a QR code. Gary scans it. It links to the lien.
- **60×:** The entire house shudders on its slab — the whole structure was collateralized to the frame. Gary's flatbed, now stacked with the Lambo, the TV, the bike, the mailbox, the hose, the numbers and the porch, is longer than the truck.
- **100×:** Gary looks at the degen at the window. The degen looks at Gary. Gary takes off his vest, folds it, and sets it on the mailbox post he left. Some things you don't tow. The vest stays.

## Suspense layer

Tension is `1 − 1/x` on the displayed multiplier (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×), so it builds through the 1×–3× window where most rounds end, with a slower log driver keeping long rounds changing. All suspense is keyed to the displayed multiplier and elapsed time, never to the outcome.

- **The blinds.** One slat open at 1×, two by 5×, fully open by 10×. The degen's silhouette opens up with tension, and his coffee mug in the window is full at 1× and empty by 8×.
- **The chart is green the entire round.** The laptop glow on the ceiling never changes color until the crash. The suspense is the gap between the chart and the driveway.
- **Gary's checklist** fills with check marks, each one a soft tick the degen hears through the floor.
- **The truck's beacons** stay off the whole round. They come on, all four, one second before the crash. This is the tell.
- **The kid's bedroom light** comes on at 5× and stays on. A small figure watches from a lower window. Gary waves.
- **The neighbor's porch light** comes on at 8× and goes off when the neighbor sees it is just Gary. Everyone knows Gary.
- A quiet heartbeat under the idling diesel quickens with tension until the player cashes out.

## Accepted cash-out

The shell confirms the exit; only a confirmed `cashoutX100` triggers this.

The degen gets the confirmation (screen flash **SOLD**), runs down the stairs in socks, out the door, and flags Gary down before the hook tightens: he buys it all back at the multiplier. Gary undoes the hook. They shake on it. Gary writes something new on the clipboard — a referral code — and the degen, in socks, at 4 am, watches his lawn get un-watered by a man he now respects. The badge reads **PAID IN FULL · GARY'S BOY NOW**.

If the chart keeps going without you, the caption keeps score: **KEPT THE HOUSE. KEPT GARY.** Everything stays; the sprinklers run on; Gary's beacons stay off; Gary drinks his coffee on the tailgate, and the house settles one board at a time. A crash after a cash-out reads as dodged: **RUG DODGED — HOOK NEVER TIGHTENED.**

## The crash

Keyed to `view.crashAge`, deterministic from the crash point so a replay itemizes identically.

1. The beacons come on, all four, a full second of amber before anything else moves.
2. The degen, watching his life get itemized, finally looks at the laptop. He was so busy watching Gary that the chart fell without him. The laptop fan screams once. The chart goes red in the bedroom's green light.
3. Downstairs, Gary hears it. Gary does not look up. Gary takes the front door off its hinges with one pull, because it was on the list, under "fixtures," the whole time.
4. The fridge jingle plays one last time, cheerfully, from the flatbed.
5. The for-sale sign lights up: **UNDER CONTRACT**. The realtor's face does not change.
6. The kid's light goes off.
7. Gary's truck pulls away with the house's front door riding shotgun, seatbelted. The door's little window mirrors the degen's window all the way down the block.
8. The chart on the laptop goes to zero. The bedroom is dark. The Post-it stays on the door frame: "you were 407 days late."

A short blink to black covers the street resetting for the next round. A crash after a cash-out is the same take played small from the tailgate, coffee in hand.

## Sound

- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here). An idling diesel that opens up with the multiplier.
- Cues: the hook click (`clang`), checklist ticks (`tick`), the fridge jingle (`notify`), the blinds' slats (`creak`), the kid's light (nothing), the beacons on the crash plus the door coming off its hinges (`door`), the truck's departure (`engine`), and a single bass drop on the chart going red.

## Lines not to cross

- Gary, the degen, the kid, the neighbor and the realtor are generic cartoons with no likeness to any real person. No real bank, lender, realty brand or towing company.
- Comedy only: no one is threatened, no one is touched, nothing is broken that does not belong to the round's own gag. The kid is never a target of the joke; Gary waving to them is the joke.
- The degen's loss is the punchline, never his family's fear. No eviction imagery beyond the round's own checklist.
- Credits have no monetary value. Nothing in the game implies the player's own money is at stake, and the label names no opponent.

## Builder's notes

This skeleton copies the [Hello World](../hello-world) template verbatim: `main.ts`, `audio.ts` and the shared CSS block are unmodified shared-shell copies; `scene.ts` is the commented template with its circle and greeting, fully functional for every phase. The build, replay fixture (`replay.json`'s `gameId` names this slug) and recorded example are already wired.

To build the game on this skeleton, work in `scene.ts` against the `SceneView` contract documented in the template's [README](../hello-world/README.md#where-to-work): render `waiting`, `betting`, `running` and `crashed` from `view.phase`, use `view.currentX100` for the multiplier (150 means 1.50×), `view.elapsed` for running animation and `view.crashAge` for the ending. Pacing matters more than anything else: about half of all rounds end before 2× (9 seconds in) and two in three before 3×, so the early checklist beats arrive every few seconds through 3× and a slower `log10` driver keeps long rounds changing. Tension is presentation only — never derive it from the crash point. Every animation is a pure function of the view, so replays, seeks and late entries draw the same frame; cues fire on confirmed changes only, and a late entry shows the current state without replaying an old cash-out or crash. The crash keys off `view.crashAge`. Keep `main.ts`, `audio.ts` and the shared CSS block untouched; shared changes belong in [`scripts/shell/`](../../scripts/shell/).

Read [the animation reference](../../docs/animation.md) before adding or changing character animation: the blinds are a slat rig that opens on a spring, the flatbed stack is a growing pile with stack physics, the for-sale sign is a sapling timelapse, and the house's items leave stage-left on a seeded order. Check waiting, running, crash, accepted cash-out, replay and small-screen behavior when relevant, then run `npm run build`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run check` and `npm test`.
