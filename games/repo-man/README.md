# Repo Man

A suburban cul-de-sac at 4:07 am under a deep blue sky: a sodium streetlight, porch lights, a thin moon. A two-story house, one window lit green upstairs. On the driveway apron, a leased lime-green supercar (a generic wedge, no badge) with a paper dealer plate. At the curb, a red flatbed tow truck idling, its four amber beacons off (for now), and **Gary**: cap, mustache, clipboard, coffee thermos, reflector vest over the company shirt (the cab door says **GARY'S TOWING & LIQUIDATION**), a barcode tattooed down his forearm. Upstairs, the **degen** at the window, blinds open one slat, his coffee mug on the sill, laptop open on the bed behind him, chart green.

The multiplier is how much Gary reclaims. Each milestone he unbolts more of the house and stacks it on his flatbed. He has a checklist. The checklist is the loan agreement, and it is on screen: Gary's clipboard in the corner fills with check marks as the round climbs.

## Direction

The comedy is Gary: a man doing a terrible job with complete dignity and a form for everything. The horror is the degen's, and it is self-inflicted — the chart behind him is green the entire round, and that is the joke. Nothing is funny or sad like a man watching his own house get itemized by someone holding a clipboard and being entirely correct.

- Gary speaks from the clipboard. He is respectful. He is thorough. He is the most reasonable person in the game.
- The degen has no lines until the cash-out, and then only one.
- The house reacts with physics: the TV leaves a patch, the porch was modular, the sprinklers run on a schedule nobody owns anymore, and everything Gary takes lands on a pile that sways on the flatbed.
- Gary never gloats. He nods. Respect between professionals.

## Dialogue ladder

Keyed to the displayed multiplier. Gary's lines are from the clipboard, queued so a bubble stays up at least 2.6 seconds before the same speaker's next line replaces it. Each rung also hands Gary a job: he walks to the item, takes it, carries it back and heaves it onto the pile, and the clipboard gets a new line, ticked when the job is done. If the curve outruns him he works faster, and he never falls more than five jobs behind.

- **Betting:** Gary sits in the cab and thumbs through the contract by dome light: "Collateral: vehicle. Secondary: fixtures. Tertiary: the vibe." "The vibe" is already the first line on his checklist. When the round starts he climbs out and walks round to the back of the truck.
- **1.2×:** He crouches and the hook settles onto the supercar's frame with a click the degen hears through the glass (he flinches): "Hook's on the frame. Collateral: vehicle."
- **1.5×:** Gary checks the contract again, turns a page, runs the highlighter along it: "There's a clause for the garage door opener. It's highlighted."
- **1.9×:** Gary works the winch lever. The supercar's nose rises an inch. The alarm chirps twice, headlights flashing, and gives up: "Up an inch. Alarm's giving up. Battery's the bank's too."
- **2.05×:** The deck tilts down to the apron and the winch pulls the supercar up onto the flatbed. It is the bottom of the pile.
- **2.3×:** Gary unhooks the garden hose from its reel and coils it over his shoulder: "Secondary: fixtures. The hose."
- **2.8×:** The TV through the living-room window goes dark mid-paragraph and comes off the wall with a suction pull. The wall patch is shaped like a mount: "The TV. You were mid-paragraph. I'll note the page."
- **3.4×:** The kid's bike, leaning under the kid's window. Gary pauses. He writes something on the clipboard. He takes the bike anyway. He ties a bow on the handlebars — there is a gift wrap roll standing against his truck's headache rack — because Gary is not a monster: "The kid's bike. …I'm adding a bow. I'm not a monster."
- **4.1×:** The smart fridge's screen — the one showing a family photo — displays a firmware notice, a blue check, and the fridge says it in its own bubble: "♪ OWNERSHIP TRANSFERRED ♪". It plays a jingle. The jingle is cheerful. This is the worst sound in the game. Gary wheels the fridge out of the front door on a dolly.
- **5×:** The degen is now at the window with two slats held open, laptop still on the bed behind him, chart still green. Gary looks up at him. Gary nods: "Morning." The kid's bedroom light comes on; at 5.4× Gary waves, and the kid waves back.
- **6×:** "Mailbox. Your mail is my mail. Respectfully." The post stays.
- **7×:** The sprinkler heads pop up and run their morning cycle on a schedule the degen no longer owns: "Sprinklers run at four. Schedule's the bank's now." The lawn waters itself for the bank.
- **8×:** The neighbour's porch light comes on; a figure in a robe looks out: "…oh. It's just Gary." Gary: "Morning, Pat." The light goes off again.
- **10×:** Gary unscrews the house numbers: 4, 0, 7. He leaves a Post-it on the door frame: "you were 407 days late." "Numbers. Four, oh, seven. I left you a note."
- **14×:** The for-sale sign grows from the lawn like a sapling in a timelapse — a sprout, two leaves, a post, an arm, the panel unfurling — the realtor's face already smiling: "Sign's coming up nice this year."
- **22×:** Gary hooks the winch cable to the porch. The porch comes off in one piece, slides across the lawn and climbs onto the pile: "The porch. It was modular. Who knew."
- **36×:** With the porch gone, the foundation shows the QR code the concrete contractor stamped into it. Gary crouches and scans it: → LIEN. "Foundation's got a QR code. …It links to the lien."
- **45×:** "Gutters. Long ones." They go across the top of the pile.
- **60×:** The entire house shudders on its slab — the whole structure was collateralized to the frame. Gary's flatbed, stacked with the supercar, the hose, the TV, the bike, the fridge, the mailbox, the numbers, the porch and the gutters, is longer than the truck: "Collateralized to the frame. My load's longer than my truck."
- **100×:** Gary walks to the mailbox post he left, takes off his vest, folds it, and sets it on the post. "Some things you don't tow. The vest stays." The vest stays.
- **Past 100×:** Gary keeps itemizing, one entry every 1.35× of multiplier, forever, each one written down and ticked on the clipboard, each one shuddering the house on its slab: the wifi password ("It was on the fridge. The fridge is on my truck."), the vibe (re-noted), the dog's name (there is no dog), the sunrise (the lien is dated six a.m.), the moon in the birdbath, the HOA vote, the doorbell chime, the smell of your coffee, "number go up", Tuesday (the feeling), your high score, the echo in the hallway.

## Suspense layer

Tension is `1 − 1/x` on the displayed multiplier (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×), so it builds through the 1×–3× window where most rounds end, with a slower log driver keeping long rounds changing. All suspense is keyed to the displayed multiplier and elapsed time, never to the outcome.

- **The blinds.** A slat rig on springs: one slat held open at 1×, a second pulled down at 2.5× and fully by 5×, a third at 7×, and the whole blind raised on its cord between 8.5× and 10×. The degen's fingers hold the slats; his face opens up with tension, leaning in. His coffee mug on the sill is full and steaming at 1× and empty by 8×.
- **The chart is green the entire round.** The laptop glow on the ceiling, on the blinds and on the siding around the window never changes color until the crash. The suspense is the gap between the chart and the driveway.
- **Gary's checklist** fills with check marks in the corner, each one a soft tick the degen hears through the floor (he flinches at every one).
- **The truck's beacons** stay off the whole round. They come on, all four, as the crash's first beat: half a second of amber before anything else moves. That is the tell, and it never comes early — the scene does not know the crash point.
- **The kid's bedroom light** comes on at 5× and stays on. A small figure with a plush bunny watches from the lower window. Gary waves. The kid waves back.
- **The neighbour's porch light** comes on at 8× and goes off when the neighbour sees it is just Gary. Everyone knows Gary.
- A quiet heartbeat under the lo-fi idle quickens with tension until the player cashes out.

## Accepted cash-out

The shell confirms the exit; only a confirmed `cashoutX100` triggers this.

The laptop flashes **SOLD** (the whole window goes white and SOLD is stamped across it, the degen's arms in the air), he leaves the window, his silhouette crosses the landing window on the way down, and the front door bangs open: he runs across the lawn in his socks, arms waving, and flags Gary down. His one line: "WAIT. I sold. I'm buying it all back." Gary takes the hook off (if the supercar was already on the deck, the deck tilts and it rolls back down to the apron). They shake on it. Everything Gary took flies home one item at a time, whatever was in his hands first. Gary writes something new on the clipboard — "Referral code GARY-407. Ten percent off your next repo." — then sits on the tailgate with his thermos, and the degen stands on the lawn in his socks at 4 am with his hands in his hoodie pocket. The badge reads **PAID IN FULL · GARY'S BOY NOW**, and the clipboard is stamped PAID IN FULL.

If the chart keeps going without you, the caption keeps score: **KEPT THE HOUSE. KEPT GARY.** Everything stays; the checklist stops; the sprinklers still run on their schedule; Gary's beacons stay off; Gary drinks his coffee on the tailgate and talks once the deal is done ("Chart's still green up there. You did fine, kid.", "Coffee's hot. Thermos is mine, before you ask.", "Lawn looks good. Not my lawn. Not my department.", …), and the house settles one board at a time. A crash after a cash-out reads as dodged: **RUG DODGED — HOOK NEVER TIGHTENED**, stamped KEPT THE DOOR.

## The crash

Keyed to the scene's own crash clock (it follows `view.crashAge`), deterministic from the crash point so a replay itemizes identically, and front-loaded: beats 1–4 land inside two seconds, every beat and both of Gary's lines by about five, and the final image is complete by 5.8 s.

1. **0 s.** The beacons come on, all four, with a soft siren, an amber flash, a hit-stop, a shake and a punch-in on the truck: half a second of amber before anything else moves.
2. **0.5 s.** The degen, watching his life get itemized, finally looks at the laptop. He was so busy watching Gary that the chart fell without him. The laptop fan screams once. The chart goes red in the bedroom's green light, and the ceiling, the blinds and the siding go red with it. The bass drop (the stinger) lands here.
3. **About 1.2 s.** Downstairs, Gary hears it. Gary does not look up. He sets down whatever he was carrying, walks to the porch and takes the front door off its hinges with one pull, because it was on the list, under "fixtures," the whole time: "Front door. Fixtures. It was on the list the whole time." The hinge screws pop out onto the porch (seeded from the crash point, so a replay drops them the same way). The Post-it goes on the frame if it was not there already.
4. **1.7 s.** The fridge jingle plays one last time, cheerfully, notes rising from the flatbed (from the kitchen, if the round never got the fridge onto the truck).
5. **2.2 s.** The for-sale sign lights up: **UNDER CONTRACT**. The realtor's face does not change. (In a round that never reached 14×, the sign sprouts first, in a hurry.)
6. **2.6 s.** The kid's light goes off (in a short round the beacons woke the kid and the light came on, so it can go off).
7. **About 3.2 s.** Gary has carried the door to the cab; he drives away with it riding shotgun, seatbelted, "Have a good one." The door's little window mirrors the degen's window, red, all the way down the block. The pile on the flatbed leans back as the truck pulls away; a supercar that was hooked but never loaded is towed off behind it. The truck is gone by about 5 s.
8. **About 4.5 s.** The chart on the laptop reaches the bottom. The bedroom goes dark; the degen slumps. The Post-it stays on the door frame, and at 5 s it is called out large enough to read: "you were 407 days late." The clipboard is stamped REPOSSESSED.

Live, the next betting window opens about two seconds after the crash. A crash this scene saw live keeps playing over the start of betting as an encore — the crash's own clock running on real time, its lines, cues, caption, stamp and multiplier held, the music kept stopped — until the ending is complete (5.8 s) or 3.5 s have passed, whichever is first, or at once if the next round starts running. Then a short blink to black covers the street resetting for the next round. A scene opened in the betting window, or on a crash it did not see, never encores.

A crash after a cash-out is the same take played small from the tailgate, coffee in hand: the beacons over Gary's head, an inset in the corner — MEANWHILE, UPSTAIRS — where the empty bedroom's chart goes red, the fridge jingle from the kitchen, the kid's light going off, and Gary raising his thermos to the degen: "Chart went red. You're down here. Good call." … "Hook never tightened." The truck stays.

## Sound

- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here), `pageAudio({ style: 'lofi', crash: 'thud' })`: a lo-fi idle that tightens with the multiplier.
- Cues: the hook settling (`clang`), checklist ticks (`tick`), the fridge jingle (`notify` and a `ding`), the blinds' slats (`creak`), the winch (`ratchet`), the alarm's two chirps (`beep`), the TV's suction pull and the bow (`pop`), each item landing on the pile (`thud`), the toss (`whoosh`), the sprinklers (`spray`, `hiss`), the neighbour's light (`click`), the QR scan (`beep`), the house shuddering (`creak`, `thud`); the kid's light makes no sound. The crash: the beacons (`siren`, soft), the stinger and the laptop fan (`buzz`) on the chart going red, the door coming off its hinges (`door`, `clang`), the truck's departure (`engine`). The exit: the cash-out register, the front door, sock squeaks, the hook, the handshake, a whoosh for every item flying home. A heartbeat until the exit, and the milestone dings.

## Lines not to cross

- Gary, the degen, the kid, the neighbour and the realtor are generic cartoons with no likeness to any real person. No real bank, lender, realty brand, car maker or towing company; the supercar has no badge.
- Comedy only: no one is threatened, no one is touched, nothing is broken that does not belong to the round's own gag. The kid is never a target of the joke; Gary waving to them is the joke.
- The degen's loss is the punchline, never his family's fear. No eviction imagery beyond the round's own checklist.
- Credits have no monetary value. Nothing in the game implies the player's own money is at stake, and the label names no opponent.

- `house.ts`: the cached sky and street, the cached house with its windows punched out, the rooms behind the glass (the bedroom with the blinds slat rig, the mug and the laptop; the living room with the TV and the smart fridge; the kid's room; the landing), the front door, the house numbers, the Post-it and its callout, the gutter, the hose, the slab and its QR code, the window light spilling onto the siding, the neighbour's house and porch light, the streetlight, the sprinklers, the for-sale sign growing like a sapling, the mailbox post.

- `cast.ts`: Gary's rig (a pose built from his act as world targets, two-bone IK arms and legs, distance-driven feet, springs on his hands, head and crouch; the cap, the mustache, the vest, the barcode tattoo, the clipboard, the pen, the thermos, the phone; Gary in the cab), the degen behind the blinds and outside in his socks, the kid and the neighbour.

The blinds, the mug, the sprinklers, the lights, the sign, the captions, the ladder and the jobs all follow the displayed multiplier; the regret ladder follows the multiplier against the accepted exit; the crash follows the scene's crash clock (which follows `crashAge`), seeded from the crash point; nothing drawn here reads or changes the committed outcome, and nothing happens before the crash that would hint at it. The truck's drive away, the pile leaning back and the hinge screws are closed-form functions of the crash clock, so a late look at the crash agrees with what a live viewer saw. A scene that opens mid-round settles into place: every job below the multiplier done and stacked, the checklist written and ticked (the last few overtime items too), Gary at his spot, the blinds and the sprinklers where the round has them; a scene that opens after an exit shows everything home and Gary on the tailgate; one that opens on a crash shows the aftermath without its cues. Long rounds stay bounded: the checklist keeps its last 40 lines, the bubbles and the job queue are capped, the pile has nine pieces, and every loop is per frame and constant. The cut from a crashed round to the next betting window goes through a short blink to black. Sound is opt-in through the Sound button.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents a close-up of the pile, Gary and the bedroom window (the truck and the front door during the crash, the lawn during the exit), a small overview that keeps the whole street, and the current line from the actual dialogue in place of the bubbles. The canonical shell still owns controls, round state and accepted cash-outs.
