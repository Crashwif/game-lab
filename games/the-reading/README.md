# The Reading

A wood-panelled study, four in the afternoon, rain on the window. A long mahogany table, a portrait of **Grandma** over the fireplace — knitted cardigan, reading glasses on a chain, holding a copy of the chart with one candle circled in red pen (the estate's only accurate prophecy). Her armchair by the fire, still warm, and a coffee table with the brown envelope the will came in under a glass paperweight that reads **WHEN**. On the walls: the deer head **Gerald**, a framed sampler (**A PLACE FOR EVERYTHING**), a hunting cabinet of duck decoys, and the kitchen door, through which the freezer hums with its light on.

The relatives, around the table, all generic cartoons: **the son** (Dad, blazer, moustache, expectations), **the daughter** (Mom, cardigan, holding a eulogy she will not get to give), **the twins** (matching sweaters, sheepish), an empty chair where **the nephew** should be (he is in a crypto hoodie and a backwards cap on a pouf at the coffee table — late, was at the wake, at the wrong table), and **the notary's assistant** (neutral, has seen everything, face like a closed envelope). At the head of the table, standing: **Peterson** the family lawyer — rumpled, bi-focaled, and the only person enjoying this. At the near end, seen from behind, **you**.

The will is the game. The estate is a portfolio. Grandma did not own what the family thought she owned: she owned what the family bought.

The multiplier is the value of the estate, and every step up the curve melts one more item: each bequest lands on the table as it is read, with a lot sticker, and slumps into a puddle that runs to the table's edge and drips onto the rug as the curve climbs past it.

## Direction

The comedy is the collision of a death's solemn inventory with the market that grandma's portfolio turns out to have been. Every bequest is read like a valuation and grieved like a keepsake; by the end the family is crying over tokens and the lawyer is crying over the fee. Grandma is the best character in the game and never appears on screen except in oils: the portrait's eyes follow the reading, the sampler's missing word is the round's thesis, and every item she left is funnier than the last because she chose it while competent.

- Peterson reads from the will. He does not editorialize. He editorializes with one eyebrow, which lifts on every line and climbs on the ones that need it.
- Dad and Mom grieve in real estate: every bequest is a room of the house.
- The nephew is the game's only critic of the estate, and he is right, and nobody listens to him.
- Gerald the deer head hangs over the whole thing. He has seen three readings. He is tired: his eyes are half-lidded and his ears droop as the round goes long.

## Dialogue ladder

Keyed to the displayed multiplier. Each bequest is read from the will, a speech bubble and a page turn, queued so a bubble stays up at least 2.6 seconds before the same speaker's next line replaces it. The early rungs come faster than anyone can read, so the reading runs a few seconds behind the curve through them and catches up by item fifteen; a line a fast curve pushes out of the queue still puts its bequest on the table. The caption is the last captioned line read.

- **Betting:** Peterson unfolds the will: "Dearly — we are here for the reading of the last will and testament of the late Margaret Holdern, who we are told kept excellent records. Item one."
- **1.15×:** "Item one: to my son, the house. To my son's debt, the house: it is not actually available, as it is collateral. Moving on." A little house lands in front of Dad, who jolts with hope.
- **1.35×:** "Item two: to my daughter, my service for twelve. Twelve of what is not specified. She will know." Mom: "Twelve. The good dining room. She meant the good dining room."
- **1.55×:** "Item three: to my grandson, my bags." Three sacks land at the nephew's empty chair. Dad: "Her what?" Peterson, one eyebrow: "Bags. Spelled out in full. She was competent."
- **1.8×:** "The bags are held in a wallet whose recovery phrase is hidden where I always hid the good chocolate." The whole family turns to the kitchen; a cousin, off-screen: "Checking the drawers!"
- **2.1×:** "Item four: to the twins, my car. I say 'car'. It is a lease. The lease is in the wallet with the bags." A toy car with a LEASE tag.
- **2.4×:** "Item five: the sampler completes. The missing word is 'dumping'." An ITEM 5 tag swings onto the sampler; Mom's eulogy is quietly folded and put away.
- **2.8×:** "Item six: Gerald. Whoever keeps Gerald keeps the house's walls. Gerald is structural." Everyone looks up; an ITEM 6 tag hangs from his antler.
- **3.2×:** "Item seven: my synthetic grandmother, to whoever is newest to the family. She is not a grandmother. She is a chatbot with my voice." A 2019 smart display lands at your end of the table and boots: "HELLO. I AM A CHATBOT FROM 2019, MADE SO SHE COULD ATTEND TWO THINGS AT ONCE. HOW CAN I HELP YOU GRIEVE?"
- **3.8×:** "Item eight: my timeshare, which is in three countries, none of which I visited, one of which does not exist." Dad: "Which one has the guest room?"
- **4.5×:** "Item nine: the crypto hoodie my grandson leaves at my house. I am leaving it back to him, washed. It is worth more than the bags." The nephew: "Correct, honestly."
- **5.5×:** "Item ten: my subscription to the genealogy site, so the family can find out where the money came from. It came from the money." Mom: "It came from the conservatory. I know it did."
- **7×:** "Item eleven: to the notary's assistant, my condolences, and a week of paid leave, effective the moment this is read aloud." The assistant: "Noted." — stands, takes the folder and leaves through the kitchen door, and an ON LEAVE note stays at their place.
- **9×:** "Item twelve: the daily paper, so that someone in this room knows what the market did without opening an app." The nephew: "I know what the market did. Nobody asked."
- **12×:** "Item thirteen: whatever is in the freezer. It is not meat. Do not open it. Do not *not* open it." An ITEM 13 tag on the freezer door. Dad: "Nobody open the freezer."
- **18×:** "Item fourteen: my recovery phrase, in case the chocolate has been found: it is not in the chocolate. Read item one again." From the kitchen: "Found the chocolate! It's… just chocolate."
- **25×:** "Item fifteen, final: to the whole family, the chart. It is the only thing I ever actually owned, and now, so do you." A framed chart stands in the middle of the table.
- **Past 25×, every 1.35× of multiplier, forever:** seventeen codicils and asides in turn — "Codicil one: the estate has gone up again. Nobody is to say 'gains' at the table.", Dad's guest room going up, the chatbot offering a recipe because it cannot hold assets, Mom giving the eulogy in her head, the nephew reading the chart aloud for the record, "Gerald is not to be consulted. Gerald has been consulted.", the twins asking if it is rude to ask if they are rich, the cousin with every drawer open, "my fee schedule is attached, and has been since item one", the nephew straight from the wake ("Different Gerald. Same chart."), and "the estate continues to appreciate. So do I. Moving on."

## Suspense layer

Tension is `1 − 1/x` on the displayed multiplier (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×), so it builds through the 1×–3× window where most rounds end, with a slower log driver keeping long rounds changing. All suspense is keyed to the displayed multiplier and elapsed time, never to the outcome.

- **The portrait's eyes** follow the reading, and at every bequest they glance at the relative named (a soft tone). At high tension they glance at the frame's edge, where the freezer is and the chart will appear at the crash.
- **The sampler's missing word** is covered by a doily on one pin. The doily drifts an inch along the pin with tension, letting the edge of a letter show. Nobody touches it.
- **The nephew's chart** on his phone is the only market data on screen: it is the game's own tell, a green line and a percentage that tick with the round, and it is green until the crash. The family does not look at it.
- **The freezer**, visible through the kitchen door, hums. The hum lines thicken and it shivers as the tension rises, with an occasional hiss. Its light is on. Nobody has opened it.
- **The recovery phrase hunt** in the kitchen happens off-screen: drawers slam (a SLAM at the door, which rattles, and the twins flinch), and the slams keep time with the reading.
- **The fireplace** burns lower as the estate melts, and the light in the room cools with it. At high tension the fire is out, a thread of smoke rises, and the armchair has gone cold and frosted.
- **The table** fills with bequests that melt one after another, with an inventory readout (ITEM 07 OF 15 · ESTATE MELTED 64%, then the codicil count).
- A quiet heartbeat under the rain quickens with tension until the player cashes out.

## Accepted cash-out

The shell confirms the exit; only a confirmed `cashoutX100` triggers this.

Your bequest: the player's seat at the table accepts item fourteen — the recovery phrase hunt — and the badge reads **ACCEPTED · NOT IN THE CHOCOLATE** with the exit multiplier and the credits under it. Peterson says so, the player stands, walks the length of the table to him, is handed the will, and walks out with it under an arm: the only family member who has read the document. Peterson reads on from a COPY.

If the chart keeps going without you, the caption keeps score: **WILL UNDER ARM**, then **THE ESTATE MELTS. THE READING CONTINUES.** The bequests continue without the player's seat, the family argues ("They took the will? We were using that."), the portrait keeps its counsel, and the chatbot announces that your relative has left the chat. A crash after a cash-out reads as dodged: **RUG DODGED — WILL UNDER ARM**, stamped **DODGED THE LIEN**.

## The crash

Keyed to the scene's crash clock (the view's `crashAge`, carried on through the encore below), deterministic from the crash point so a replay melts identically. The beats are front-loaded for the live hold, which moves on to the next betting phase about two seconds after the crash: beats 1 to 4 land inside two seconds, every beat by five, and the final image is complete at six.

1. The nephew's chart goes red in one frame (the camera punches in on the phone, with a flash and a hit-stop). He looks up from it, the only person in the room who knows what happened, and he does not say anything. He is not asked.
2. Peterson puts the will down on the table and holds up a filing he has been sitting on since the first bequest: every bequest today carries a lien. One by one, every kept item on the table — the service, the lease, the washed hoodie, the genealogy subscription, and the envelope under WHEN — is stamped **COLLATERAL**. The family keeps nothing. The bank keeps everything.
3. The sampler's doily comes off its pin on its own and flutters down onto the table. The completed sentence reads **A PLACE FOR EVERYTHING, AND EVERYTHING IS COLLATERAL**.
4. Grandma's portrait's eyes do one thing they have not done all round: they close.
5. The freezer's door, unattended, swings open by itself in a breath of cold. Inside: ice trays of frozen chart lines, red pen and all. Dad: "She froze the dip." Mom: "She was competent."
6. Peterson, fee schedule up, dries one tear with the will's envelope: the only family member who ends the round richer.
7. Gerald the deer head, structural, stays on the wall. The walls come off around him, panel by panel in a seeded order, the furthest first, and the studs fall after them, leaving him on the last stud in the rain. He blinks once. He has seen this before.
8. The fire's last pop. The chair is cold. The rain stops. The room is dark but for the freezer's cold light. The will, on the table, unrolls into a receipt, total 0.00. "That concludes the reading. Your receipt."

If the next betting phase arrives while the ending is still playing, and this scene saw the crash live, the ending plays on as an encore on the scene's own clock (real frame time; at most 3.5 s, and cut short if the next round starts running): the crash caption, the stamp, the crash multiplier and the crashed music hold, the crash lines and cues continue, and no betting cue plays. Then a short blink to black covers the study resetting for the next round, and Peterson unfolds the will again. A crash after a cash-out is the same melt seen small through the doorway from the hall, with you in the foreground, will under arm. If the round busts before anything is read, the filing is read over the unread will.

## Sound

- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here) in its `ambient` style — a slow dorian pad and a sine lead in a large room that open up with the multiplier — with the `thud` crash stinger and tape stop.
- `weather.ts`: the study's own bed on the shared bus: rain on the glass that swells with the tension and stops at the end of the crash, and the fire's crackle, thinning as it burns down.
- Cues: Peterson's page turns (`whoosh`), every other line (`pop`, the chatbot `beep`), a bequest landing (`thud`) and the portrait's glance (a soft `ding`), the tags (`tick`), the drawer slams in the kitchen (`thud`), the freezer's hiss (`hiss`), the assistant's leave (`door`), bequest stamps on milestones (`milestone`), the doily's move (nothing), the heartbeat (`heartbeat`) until the exit, the hand-over (`whoosh`) and the door (`door`) on the way out; and the crash: the filing's `thud`, a `stomp` per COLLATERAL stamp, the freezer's `creak` and `hiss`, a `creak` or `clang` per wall panel, the fire's last `pop`, the receipt's `ratchet`.

## Lines not to cross

- Every relative, the lawyer, the assistant and the portrait are generic cartoons with no likeness to any real person. Margaret Holdern is fictional, and Gerald the deer head is no relation to anyone else called Gerald.
- Comedy only: no death is shown, no body, no funeral — the death is off-screen, recent, and the game begins after it. The grief is genuine and the family is not mocked for it; the portfolio is.
- The synthetic grandmother is a chatbot gag about 2019 tech — a smart display with a cartoon face that talks in capitals and is labelled a chatbot every time it speaks — not a person, and is never mistaken for one by the game.
- Credits have no monetary value. Nothing in the game implies the player's own money is at stake, and the label names no opponent.

## How it is built

- `scene.ts`: the clocks (real `dt` clamped to 0.1 s, a 0.15 s hit-stop, slow motion and a camera punch on the phone at the crash; the scene-owned crash clock and the encore into betting), tension and the log driver, the confirmed exit, phase edges with late entry and quiet crashes, the dialogue's prop beats (each bequest placed when its line is said, or quietly when a fast curve dropped the line), the bubble layout (each bubble placed once, beside its speaker, by a search that keeps it inside the frame and off the HUD, every face and every other bubble, and off Grandma's face, the fire, the doily and the phone where it can), the cues, the draw order, the hallway framing after an exit, the HUD (caption, badge, multiplier, the inventory readout, the outcome stamp) and the portrait compositor.
- `study.ts`: the set. The static room (panelling, window, sampler, cabinet, kitchen, floor and rug, chimney breast, Grandma in oils) is painted once into a sprite at the frame's pixel density; live on top: the rain on the glass, the fire and its sparks and glow, Grandma's two-dot eyes with a look-at target and their lids, the doily on its pin (swing, drift and the fall), Gerald (sprites for the plaque and face, live ears and lids, one blink), the freezer (hum, light, the door's swing and the frozen dip), the kitchen door's rattle and the slams, the armchair going cold, the table, the coffee table with WHEN, the lighting, and the crash's seeded panel removal and falling studs, the outdoor rain and the dark; plus the hallway.
- `family.ts`: the cast. The seated family as a reaction crowd (a lean spring per member at its own rate, a hunch with the tension, a head that lags and turns toward whoever they attend to, blinks, worry, jolts on their own bequests, Mom's tears and folded eulogy, Dad's sweat, the twins' sideways glances, two-bone IK arms resting on the table), the assistant's walk-out, Peterson (the will with page turns, bifocals, the one eyebrow, IK arms that hand over the will and at the crash put it down, raise the filing and dab the tear), the nephew (cap, hoodie, the phone and its chart, looking up at the crash), and the player from behind, on a distance-driven gait with IK legs for the walk to Peterson and out, and in the hall.
- `estate.ts`: the words (ladder, codicils, betting line, captions, regrets, crash lines, stamps) and the inventory: each bequest's art, where it sits and whom Grandma glances at, the melt (`log(x / read-at) / log 3.2`), puddles, runs and drips, lot stickers, the COLLATERAL stamp and the receipt.
- `weather.ts`: the rain bed and the crackle on the shared audio bus.
- `script.ts`: the dialogue engine (a queue keyed to the multiplier, a 2.6 s hold per speaker, an overtime cycle, unscheduled lines, late-entry settling), with a per-line `hold` and a queue cap that drops asides before bequests. `ink.ts`: the outline, lettering, bubbles, shapes, bubble measuring and resolution-aware sprites. `motion.ts`: springs, easing, noise and the seeded generator. `kinematics.ts`: two-bone IK and the walking foot. `portrait.ts`: the narrow-screen composition.
- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (`ambient`, crash `thud`; never edited here). `main.ts`: the shared page shell. `replay.json`: a verified 9.07× round for the gallery, with this game's ID.

The family, the reading, the melt, the fire, the rain, the hum, the slams and the HUD all follow the displayed multiplier and the round clock; the crash follows the scene's crash clock (the view's `crashAge`, then real time through the encore) and is seeded from the crash point (`mulberry32(x100 · 7 + 11)`), and every crash pose — the stamps, the doily's fall, the eyes, the freezer, the panels and studs, the dark, the receipt — is a closed-form function of the crash age, so a replay or a late entry draws the same frame. Nothing drawn here reads or changes the committed outcome. A scene opened mid-round settles straight into place: every bequest below the multiplier on the table and melted as far as the multiplier says, the tags hung, the eulogy put away, the assistant gone past 7×, the hunt on past 1.8×, the last line or two up; after an exit the player is already gone with the will; after a crash the aftermath is shown as it stands with no cues. Long rounds stay bounded: fifteen bequests at most, puddles and drips that stop growing, at most five bubbles and six queued lines, and the codicils keep coming every 1.35× forever.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents a close-up of Peterson, the family and the portrait (the freezer, Gerald and the sampler at the crash, the walk-out after an exit, the doorway from the hall after a dodged crash), a small overview of the whole study, and the current line from the dialogue.
