# Not Financial Advice

A shill video shot in front of a green screen. The influencer stands in a crypto hoodie with a clip mic and shades beside a rented Lambo, in front of a badly keyed beach; the follower counter, the 1000X thumbnail arrow and the promo code spin up with the displayed multiplier, and the PAID PROMOTION disclosure in the corner shrinks by a step at every milestone until it is one pixel. Every milestone is also a sponsor read: a product springs up to camera in his pointing hand, wobbles, and is held for a moment under a SPONSOR chyron, each dumber than the last (NFT toothpaste, a hardware wallet that is a notebook, AIRDROP energy drink, diamond hands gloves, a hopium vape, a Lambo air freshener in rental scent, a generational wealth candle, a seed phrase tattoo kit). Tension: the green screen tears at the edges, the RENTAL sticker peels off the Lambo, the tow truck's lights show in his shades, PENDING SELL rows appear in the wallet on his second monitor with a COUSIN row on top dumping harder as it climbs, bot icons gather beside the follower count, and he says "not financial advice" faster and faster. An accepted cashout is your subscriber taking a screenshot (a shutter flash with the frame's corners) and leaving: UNFOLLOWED, the video minimises to a window in the corner of the desktop, and the badge reads DYOR. The crash is the reveal, cut like a record scratch: the frame freezes on the wallet posting SOLD, the green screen starts to fall in slow motion with the camera punched in on him, then time catches up: the tow truck backs in beeping and hauls the Lambo out, the follower count is struck through and stamped 98% BOTS, the comments flood with RUG and DEV SOLD, and the sponsor card replaces his face.

- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio (a copy of the shell's, never edited here). Trap that tightens with the multiplier; the crash is a record scratch. Cues: a sell queued in the wallet (`notify`), each sponsor read (`kaching`), the disclosure's next step down (`tick`), the tow truck arriving and backing in (`engine`, `beep`), the subscriber's screenshot (`camera`), the video minimising (`whoosh`), the chat turning (`boo`), plus milestones on the caption ladder and the cash-out register.
- `motion.ts`: the shared toolkit: exact damped springs, easing, deterministic noise, a seeded generator.
- `studio.ts`: the video frame: the keyed beach and its tearing edges, the garage behind it, the influencer's rig (point, nod, mouth flaps, hand shake), the sponsor reads and their chyron, the Lambo and its sticker, the wallet monitor with the cousin's row, the tow truck and its reversing beeps, the screenshot flash, the sponsor card and the minimised window, and the event edges the cues fire from.
- `overlay.ts`: the chrome around the video: the follower counter and its bots, the 1000X arrow, the promo chip, the shrinking disclosure, the comment column and its crash flood, and the subscriber row under the video with your subscriber's exit.
- `scene.ts`: composition, the crash choreography (freeze frame, slow motion, punch-in), the audio cues, the HUD over the video (captions, multiplier, the follower count, the secured badge, the outcome pop) and the round-phase logic.
- `main.ts`: SDK connection, round lifecycle, the player's bet and cashout, input controls and the animation loop.
- `replay.json`: a verifiable settled example round used by the gallery.

Fictional influencer, fictional sponsors, no real platform or person. Nothing drawn here changes the committed outcome. A scene that opens mid-round or on a crashed round settles into place (no product held up, the aftermath already there) without replaying it or playing its cues. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off the screen shake, the freeze frame and the punch-in.

## Visual direction and small screens

The scene now introduces physical presentation acts at 32, 52, 75, 100, 125 and 145 seconds, followed by bounded recurring acts for unusually long rounds. New props accompany changes in character effort, with a short easing of tension before renewed activity. `acts.ts` reads elapsed time only; it cannot choose an outcome or promise that a round will last this long.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents an enlarged character/detail view, a small overview that preserves the location, and a readable current line from the actual dialogue/chat/monitor state. The canonical shell still owns controls, round state and accepted cashouts. Reduced motion removes the new prop oscillation.
