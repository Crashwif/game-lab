# Roadster to Mars

You are the Roadster. Not the driver: there is no driver, the car was remotely operated and the crowd was kept 300 yards back for their ears. The pad is SpaceX's test site at McGregor, Texas, as on October 15, 2026: a crowd filming, the Bond Lotus parked where its owner can see it, a lit quarter-pipe, a launch beacon, and a row of Model 3, Model Y, Cybertruck (plus one panel on the ground), Semi and Cybercab to clear. While the lobby waits the car idles with its hazards blinking.

The round lifts it off on the round clock: it drives in, up the ramp, leaves the lip nose-up with the cold-gas jets lit, and over the next half second blends into the climb. Its altitude runs on the natural log of the multiplier (360 px per doubling), so the pad, the ramp and the Teslas drop away, the clouds thin out, the sun turns from a daytime glow into a hard point, the stars and a Milky Way band fade in with shooting stars, and above 4× the Earth's curvature rises into frame with an atmosphere rim, continents, clouds and a terminator, shrinks to a marble by the Moon and fades past Mars. Once the car is clear of the ramp it does one slow 360° (eased on the multiplier between 1.335× and 1.5×, with a camera punch and ghosted frames). A rung of the ladder lands a caption and a stinger every few seconds through 3× and keeps going: the Skycycle X-2 at Snake River Canyon, an FAA sign whose listed purpose is "hazards", a 737 with a contrail, Felix's balloon, the dashed Kármán line, the ISS, a Starlink train, the Moon, Starman waving from the 2018 Roadster, Mars at 100× (a flag that grows and unfurls reading 2028, actually, with a Mars stamp and a cheer), the asteroid belt with 2018 CN41 deleted after 17 hours, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto and Voyager 1, with a comet on a 14 s loop and overtime captions beyond.

An accepted cash-out (WELL, IT WORKED.) deploys a six-gore parachute labelled REFUNDABLE: your car swings down and out of the picture while a dashed, remote-operated ghost flies on so you can see where the round ends, and the ending reads a regret line scaled to what you left on the table. The crash is a rapid unscheduled disassembly: a hit-stop (0.15 s freeze, then slow motion), a flash, a shockwave ring, sparks, fire, cold gas and smoke, three car fragments and six pressure vessels tumbling with smoke trails and bouncing on the pavement if the ground is still in view, a reason seeded from the crash point, and the production year slipping (2028, actually; 2029, actually ×2). An instant crash is a launch scrubbed for weather: rain, a dark cloud deck, lightning on the clock and a sad trombone. A spectator's crash reads WATCHED IT FALL.

The HUD: the multiplier top-left, warming from white to gold to Mars orange, with the altitude in real units and a 600-litre air gauge that empties at the Kármán line and reads RUNNING ON HYPE after; the production counter; a lobby of fellow reservation holders (Sam A. waiting since 2018, Fred L. with two referral cars and zero cars, Marques B., Lars M. the 600 litres guy, Founders Series #0001, the ghost of Evel Knievel, Starman who never lands, dogwifcopv) whose landings are seeded per round and read straight off the multiplier; and a hype ticker labelled PARODY · NOT REAL POSTS that mixes lines from the event with the obvious next sentence.

**Trim** is the game's own control: Performance (no thrusters, climbs on gold hype sparkles), Thrust (two rear nozzles) and Rocket (ten perimeter nozzles, one seat, no road approval), which is invite only until a flight you watched passes 10×. It changes what the car shows and nothing else: the room's chain owns the pace. The trim, the production year, the Mars count and the invite persist in `localStorage` where a frame allows it, and never change during a replay.

- `scene.ts`: composition, the round-phase logic, the ladder, the flip, the parachute, the burst choreography, the lobby, the ticker, the audio cues and the HUD.
- `roadster.ts`: the world (sky, sun, stars, clouds, the receding Earth, the pad, the cameos, the flag), the car and its jets, the parachute, the particles and the seeded wreckage.
- `motion.ts`: springs, easing, deterministic noise and a seeded generator (the shared toolkit, copied).
- `audio.ts`: the shared page audio, never edited here. Synthwave that tightens with the multiplier; the crash is a boom, a scrub is a trombone. Cues: the engine on the drive-in, a whoosh off the lip and on the flip, a milestone at each rung, a gasp at the FAA, a cheer and a ding at Mars, a pop as the chute opens, the register on a cash-out, a buzz for the Rocket invite.
- `main.ts`: the shared page shell. `replay.json`: a verified 9.07× round for the gallery. `clips.json`: `{}`, procedural sound.

Tension is `1 − 1/x` and drives the music and the camera rumble. The pose is a pure function of the multiplier and `elapsed` (the launch on the clock, the flip on the multiplier, the flag on the multiplier), the burst keys off `crashAge` with a seeded scatter, and the lobby reads off the multiplier, so a replay, a seek and a late entry draw the same frame; only the puffs, the sparks and the parachute's fall are integrated, with a clamped step. A scene that opens mid-round or on a crashed round settles into place. The published game uses full animation regardless of browser motion preferences.

Run `npm run preview -- roadster-to-mars` from the repository root and open the printed URL; remove `?mode=replay` for live play against the emulator. Place a deposit of 50 local credits, then land it during a running round. `npm run build` produces the three publishable files in `dist/roadster-to-mars/`. All visuals are original Canvas 2D shapes and text; no third-party assets. Credits have no monetary value.
