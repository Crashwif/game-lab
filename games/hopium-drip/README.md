# Hopium Drip

A ward at Degen General. A patient in a hospital gown sits up in bed with a laptop; an IV bag labelled HOPIUM drips into his arm and drains on the multiplier curve, and the monitor beside the bed draws the chart as his heartbeat, faster with every multiple. Tension (1 − 1/x, so a third at 1.5×, half at 2× and two thirds at 3×) drives the heart, the bag, the pupils, the sweat and the music; the heart rate keeps climbing on a slower log driver through long rounds. The story lands on the displayed multiplier: the roommate says wagmi bro at 1.2×, a dose with a twitch and a chart note at 1.5×, 2×, 3× and on, the curtain is half drawn round the roommate at 1.6× while his monitor races amber, the doctor checks his watch at 2.05× (not yet...), the roommate flatlines at 2.2× (BEEEEEEP) and is wheeled out under a sheet at 2.45×, a crash cart labelled BUY THE DIP rolls in at 2.8× just in case, his wife walks in at 3.5× with DIVORCE PAPERS and a tapping foot (sign here.), and the nurse swaps the bag for COPIUM at 5×; the flowers wilt along the way. An accepted cashout is a discharge: a register and a green wash, he shuts the laptop, the sheet and blanket fold back to the foot of the bed, the drip is pulled and dangles from the stand, the gown swaps for a suit, the wife lets it go (nvm, love u) and leaves, and he walks out of the ward with a GAINS balloon, the door closing behind him. His heart calms and the doses stop; the badge reads DISCHARGED and the caption runs a jeet's regret ladder while the chart pumps on without him. If the rug comes after that, it is dodged: DODGED IT, the monitor reads LEADS OFF, and the doctor shrugs at the empty bed (discharged against medical advice, paper hands). The crash is the flatline, its key beats inside the two seconds before the next round: FLATLINE pops at once, a seeded scribble on the monitor, then the long flat line and the bag relabelled RUGGED; the doctor drops the clipboard, grabs the paddles off the cart (the monitor reads CHARGING), lunges at the laptop over a planted back foot and shouts CLEAR! twice, each shock a 0.15 s hit-stop, a white flash, a jolt in slow motion with the camera cut 10% into the bed and a single spike on the flat trace; then the paddles go down, the sheet goes over the laptop, the wife's papers read SIGNED, the lights dim and the doctor calls time of death at the crash multiplier, about 1.6 s in. Between rounds the lights go down and the ward resets in the dark. A player's bed reads bed 2 · YOU; a spectator arrives at VISITING HOURS.

- `motion.ts`: the shared toolkit: exact damped springs, easing, deterministic noise, a seeded generator.
- `audio.ts`: the page's shared procedural music and effects (the same file in every game). Hopium Drip plays hospital muzak that races with the heart rate, a heartbeat in time with the trace's beats (never faster than the trace), a beep with every dose and a glug when the bag swaps to COPIUM, a gasp when the roommate flatlines, a notification when the wife walks in, the cart's squeak, the register on the discharge and the door as he leaves, and on the crash the long beep, the CLEAR!, the zaps and the bell of the time of death.
- `ward.ts`: the ward set: the bed rig (lean, twitch, pupils), the IV stand and its draining bag, the doctor, his watch and the clipboard he drops, the crash cart and the paddles, the CLEAR! shocks, the wife and her papers, the flowers, the roommate behind the curtain, the discharge walk, the time-of-death sequence, the shrug at a dodged rug and the one-frame events the scene turns into sound. Its beats are keyed to the displayed multiplier, its oscillators run on integrated phases, and the walks are driven by distance.
- `kinematics.ts`: the gait: a planted stance foot, a swing that lands without skating, and a first step that leaves each foot where it stood.
- `monitor.ts`: the bedside monitor: the EKG trace driven by the displayed multiplier (and its beat count), the vitals, the dose ladder with its milestone pulses, the seeded flatline scribble, the long flat line, the CHARGING header and the paddles' spike.
- `scene.ts`: composition, the HUD (captions, multiplier, the dose count, the secured badge, the outcome pop), the shock choreography (hit-stop, slow motion, camera punch-in), the sound cues, the lights-out between rounds and the round-phase logic.
- `main.ts`: SDK connection, round lifecycle, the player's bet and cashout, input controls and the animation loop.
- `replay.json`: a verifiable settled example round used by the gallery.

The heartbeat, the bag level, the notes, the curtain, the roommate, the cart, the wife and the pupils all follow the displayed multiplier; nothing in the presentation reads or changes the committed outcome. Comedy only. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off the shake, the monitor flicker, the pulse flashes, the shock's flash and bolts, the hit-stop and the punch-in.

Run `npm run dev` from the repository root and open the Hopium Drip URL it prints. Join with 50 local credits, then cash out during a running round to be discharged. `npm run build` produces the three publishable files in `dist/hopium-drip/`.

## Visual direction and small screens

The scene now introduces physical presentation acts at 32, 52, 75, 100, 125 and 145 seconds, followed by bounded recurring acts for unusually long rounds. Each act's props fade in, cross-fade into the next and fade out at the crash or the cash-out; the acts never lower the tension. `acts.ts` reads elapsed time only; it cannot choose an outcome or promise that a round will last this long.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents an enlarged character/detail view, a small overview that preserves the location, and a readable current line from the actual dialogue/chat/monitor state. The canonical shell still owns controls, round state and accepted cashouts. Reduced motion removes the new prop oscillation.
