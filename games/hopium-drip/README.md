# Hopium Drip

A ward at Degen General. A patient in a hospital gown sits up in bed with a laptop; an IV bag labelled HOPIUM drips into his arm and drains on the multiplier curve, and the monitor beside the bed draws the chart as his heartbeat, faster with every multiple. Tension: the bag empties, the doctor's clipboard notes get worse, the flowers wilt, the nurse swaps the bag for COPIUM, the roommate behind the curtain flatlines first and is wheeled out, and the patient's pupils grow. Past 4× a crash cart labelled BUY THE DIP rolls in and parks by the bed, just in case; past 5× his wife comes in the door with DIVORCE PAPERS and a tapping foot (sign here.). An accepted cashout is a discharge: a register and a green wash, the drip is pulled, the gown swaps for a suit, the wife lets it go (nvm, love u) and leaves, and he walks out of the ward with a GAINS balloon, the door closing behind him, and the badge reads DISCHARGED. The crash is the flatline: a seeded scribble on the monitor, then the long flat line and the bag relabelled RUGGED; the doctor drops the clipboard, grabs the paddles off the cart (the monitor reads CHARGING), lunges at the laptop and shouts CLEAR! twice, each shock a hit-stop, a white flash, a jolt in slow motion with the camera punched into the bed and a single spike on the flat trace; then the paddles go down, the sheet goes over the laptop, the wife's papers read SIGNED, the lights dim and the doctor calls time of death at the crash multiplier.

- `motion.ts`: the shared toolkit: exact damped springs, easing, deterministic noise, a seeded generator.
- `audio.ts`: the page's shared procedural music and effects (the same file in every game). Hopium Drip plays hospital muzak that races with the heart rate, a heartbeat in time with the trace's beats (never faster than the trace), a beep with every dose and a glug when the bag swaps to COPIUM, a gasp when the roommate flatlines, a notification when the wife walks in, the cart's squeak, the register on the discharge and the door as he leaves, and on the crash the long beep, the CLEAR!, the zaps and the bell of the time of death.
- `ward.ts`: the ward set: the bed rig (lean, twitch, pupils), the IV stand and its draining bag, the doctor and the clipboard he drops, the crash cart and the paddles, the CLEAR! shocks, the wife and her papers, the flowers, the roommate behind the curtain, the discharge walk, the time-of-death sequence and the one-frame events the scene turns into sound.
- `monitor.ts`: the bedside monitor: the EKG trace driven by the displayed multiplier (and its beat count), the vitals, the dose ladder with its milestone pulses, the seeded flatline scribble, the long flat line, the CHARGING header and the paddles' spike.
- `scene.ts`: composition, the HUD (captions, multiplier, the dose count, the secured badge, the outcome pop), the shock choreography (hit-stop, slow motion, camera punch-in), the sound cues and the round-phase logic.
- `main.ts`: SDK connection, round lifecycle, the player's bet and cashout, input controls and the animation loop.
- `replay.json`: a verifiable settled example round used by the gallery.

The heartbeat, the bag level, the notes, the curtain and the pupils all follow the displayed multiplier; nothing in the presentation changes the committed outcome. Comedy only. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off the shake, the monitor flicker, the pulse flashes, the shock's flash and bolts, the hit-stop and the punch-in.

Run `npm run dev` from the repository root and open the Hopium Drip URL it prints. Join with 50 local credits, then cash out during a running round to be discharged. `npm run build` produces the three publishable files in `dist/hopium-drip/`.

## Visual direction and small screens

The scene now introduces physical presentation acts at 32, 52, 75, 100, 125 and 145 seconds, followed by bounded recurring acts for unusually long rounds. New props accompany changes in character effort, with a short easing of tension before renewed activity. `acts.ts` reads elapsed time only; it cannot choose an outcome or promise that a round will last this long.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents an enlarged character/detail view, a small overview that preserves the location, and a readable current line from the actual dialogue/chat/monitor state. The canonical shell still owns controls, round state and accepted cashouts. Reduced motion removes the new prop oscillation.
