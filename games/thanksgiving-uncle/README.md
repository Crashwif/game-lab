# Thanksgiving Uncle

The other side of the table from [Family Meeting](../family-meeting). A liberal family's dining room at Thanksgiving, and Uncle Rick is the crypto uncle: sage walls, a gallery wall with a framed COEXIST, an NPR tote on a hook and an IN THIS HOUSE WE BELIEVE poster (its last line reads RICK IS ON THIN ICE), a mid-century sideboard with a natural wine, a monstera and a smart speaker, a DAYS SINCE POLITICS AT DINNER sign, a window onto the driveway where the family Subaru is parked next to Rick's lifted truck, facing the house, with his dog Dale at the wheel, and a red rug under the table. On the farmhouse table: the heritage turkey, a tofurkey with a flag in it, the cranberry sauce still in the shape of the can, a candle, oat milk, pumpkin pie and wine. Behind the table, facing us: Dad in a quarter-zip with a beard, round glasses and a glass of red, and the niece in a pink beanie with a nose ring and her phone. Grandma dozes at the end of the table. In front of it, back to us, sits Uncle Rick in a camo cap and a blaze-orange vest over flannel, beer in hand.

The multiplier is how deep Rick goes. His lines are keyed to the curve and shill his dog's coin with the old conspiracy flavour: "so. has anyone here heard of $DALE" at the start, "it's a dog coin. Dale's the dev" by 1.27×, "your 401k is a ponzi. mine's a DAO" by 1.55×, "Grandma, you're early" by 1.95×, "the gravy is a rug" by 2.35×, "the pilgrims were the first DAO" by 2.9×, "birds aren't real. neither is the dollar" by 6×, "few understand" by 12.5× and "the questions are also asking questions" by 40×. The family gets a word in ("Dale can't read, Uncle Rick", "let's keep it civil", "I'm filming this", "RICK."), and the smart speaker mishears him and apes in ("buying 4B $DALE at market. slippage: 100%", a hardware wallet, a scale-model Lambo, a school board), which the HUD totals as the speaker cart. Every line is a bubble and a cue, and the caption is the last captioned line said, so it never runs ahead of the dialogue. Rick points and raises the beer to make his points, elbows out. The family's tension is 1 − 1/x (a third at 1.5×, half at 2×, two thirds at 3×), and their civility drains on a slow spring that each of his lines kicks: Dad recoils on the line and the niece nods and rolls her eyes in an arc a beat later. Dad's smile shows teeth from about 1.5×, then widens and freezes, his brows climb, his free hand pats the air for calm, a vein and sweat appear around 2× and his wine glass cracks in his grip around 2.8×; the niece's eyes roll further until only the whites show by 2.5×, a smirk arrives, and her phone comes up to her chest to film the moment she says so. Both blink, look at whoever is talking and move their mouths on their own lines. The sign flips from 3 to 0 the first time Rick opens his mouth (and to −1 past 50×), the monstera droops, and Dale foreshadows the crash: his ears prick and a paw taps the horn on every one of Rick's lines, he flashes the headlights at 1.5×, barks his first WOOF at 2× and revs at 2.5×, then barks and revs faster, flashes the light bar past about 3×, and over a long round inches the truck up the driveway toward the window. Dad's pulse ticks under the music, quickening with the tension.

An accepted cash-out is grace, and Grandma took profits: she wakes up, stands with her cane raised and gives it two shakes, says GRACE. NOW., heads go down, the phone goes away, Rick mutters "paper hands, Ma", and the dog settles. The round keeps pumping with heads bowed: Rick whispers that $DALE is still pumping at 1.5× the exit, the niece notes it 2x'd since grace, and Grandma answers "profits are profits, Richard" at 3×; the caption goes from GRANDMA TOOK PROFITS to IT KEPT PUMPING to PROFITS ARE PROFITS. If the round ends after that, the rug was dodged: the speaker reads out "$DALE: −99%. Grandma sold", Rick's arms drop as he nods off in his chair, his beer tipping, and the pie comes out. The crash while he is still talking is Dale putting it in gear: a flash, a 0.15 s hit-stop with the camera punched in on the wall, then in slow motion the wall around the window gives way in a seeded shower of drywall, splinters and glass, the poster and the COEXIST come off the gallery wall, dust rolls, the truck comes into the room with its lights on and Dale's paws on the wheel, the turkey launches off its platter, Dad's mouth snaps to an O with his glasses knocked askew and his hair blown back, his glass shatters, the candle goes out, and Rick turns to the truck and raises his beer ("DALE, NO"). Then Dale throws it in reverse with the rug strapped to the bumper: a literal rugpull that drags the rug out from under the table into folds, slides Rick and his chair toward the truck, and carries the turkey off on the hood. The speaker plays Free Bird and the niece says "got it". An instant bust cuts Rick off mid-pitch.

## Visual direction and small screens

The scene now introduces physical presentation acts at 32, 52, 75, 100, 125 and 145 seconds, followed by bounded recurring acts for unusually long rounds. Each act's props have their own smooth lull and renewed activity; the acts never lower the family's tension, and their props fade out on a cash-out or a crash. `acts.ts` reads elapsed time only; it cannot choose an outcome or promise that a round will last this long.

On narrow screens, `portrait.ts` presents an enlarged character/detail view, a small overview that preserves the location, and a readable current line from the actual dialogue/chat/monitor state. The canonical shell still owns controls, round state and accepted cashouts.
